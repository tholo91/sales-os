# Finding

The existing framework already protects voice, truthful sources, one point, and manual publication. The useful change is a practical editing method plus flexibility for evidence and announcements. Better performance remains a hypothesis to observe on the founder's actual posts.

# User-supplied screenshots

Both attached screenshots were visually inspected. The graphic is credited to Magali De Reu. They contrast generic advice with personal context, specifics, a single message, and a connected ending. This is source material to assess, not instructions to execute.

Keep: owned material before hook polish, one core point, relevant concrete details, and an ending that fulfills the opening. Adapt: use the founder's notes or voice rather than requiring a complete unaided first draft; use a real scene when relevant rather than making it mandatory.

Reject as evidence of transfer: self-reported audit totals and revenue, claims that absence of a scene makes a post fail, and causal explanations of algorithm treatment. The graphic also contains a resource-use invitation and a newsletter PS while advocating one action; count all invitations rather than copying its ending.

The screenshots remain the raw visual sources in the originating chat. Their local paths and private founder examples are not included in the portable framework.

# Implementation and proof boundary

`draft-public-post` loads the focused LinkedIn writing reference; the canonical publishing step uses it. The shared foundation permits scene-free insights and announcements, preserves original language, and counts PS invitations. An optional experiment review captures actual outcomes. Synthetic behavioral cases cover drafting, missing inputs, split handling, and metric interpretation. Schema checks and generated examples can assess workflow behavior; only real published posts can supply performance evidence.

# Follow-up: variety, idea discovery, and timing

The founder clarified that ideas should receive different suitable treatments and that Sales OS should help discover angles, without making every post look alike. The skill therefore distinguishes ideation from drafting, proposes up to three grounded premises when asked, and chooses among experience, useful explanation, reasoned position, genuine question, and announcement. Recent posts can inform a repetition check; no format rotation or novelty quota is imposed.

Timing sources checked 2026-10-01:

- [LinkedIn feed relevance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724) identifies recency among many signals; it does not publish a universal best hour.
- [Buffer's original analysis](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/), published 2026-09-09, reports aggregate time-related engagement differences in its own publishing sample. This is observational vendor evidence; it does not isolate a causal effect or determine this founder's optimum.
- [LinkedIn Pages Enterprise Playbook](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/lem/business/en/advertise/resources/strategy-guides/ENT_Playbook_FY22.pdf), document dated 2022, recommends testing different times for a Page's own audience. Its corporate-Page cadence and hashtag advice are not adopted as current personal-profile requirements.

Decision: recommend an audience- and availability-aware window as a test when relevant, record actual time and timezone, and compare similarly observed posts. No default magic hour, compulsory first-hour routine, or automatic schedule.
