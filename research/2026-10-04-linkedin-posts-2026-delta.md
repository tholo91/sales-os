# Summary

The repo's post guidance (`knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`, `draft-public-post`) is sound on truth, voice and one-point focus, but it is written defensively: it says what not to claim and leaves Thomas without a hook toolkit, a cadence, a comment routine, a CTA design for leads, or a bridge from post engagement to DMs. Three platform changes in 2026 matter most: (1) LinkedIn now ranks with LLM embeddings plus a sequence model of each member's reading history, so a post must be semantically unambiguous about its topic and fit Thomas's topic profile; (2) LinkedIn actively demotes "AI slop" (-40% views, reported by LinkedIn) and engagement bait, so a founder-only detail per post is now a distribution requirement, not just style; (3) organic reach per post fell sharply (practitioner data), so leads come from fewer, better posts plus daily substantive comments plus deliberate DM follow-through, not from volume.

Corrections to the repo: `draft-public-post` must stop withholding a draft when material is thin (Thomas decision 2026-10-04: always draft, mark `[PRUEFEN: ...]`); it should return two alternative opening lines with different emotional centres by default (the 2026-09-18 complaint was about the hook); and it should offer a cadence and a lead CTA when the goal is growth.

# 1. Findings that change drafting (ranked)

1. **Write so a language model can tell exactly what the post is about, and stay on 2-3 topics.** LinkedIn's March 2026 engineering post describes LLM-generated embeddings for posts and members and a transformer ranker over each member's history of what they "read, liked, commented on, returned back to, or simply scrolled past", refreshed within minutes. Practitioner van der Blom (2026) calls this the shift from relationship graph to interest graph and recommends ~80% of posts on one main topic plus at most 2-3 related subtopics (reviewer correction: the interview says "one main topic and 2-3 related subtopics", not "two main topics"). Rule: name the concrete topic in plain words in the first lines (e.g. "Brief an Abgeordnete", "Kommunalpolitik", "Bürgerbeteiligung", "Civic Tech", "Solo-Gründer"), keep Thomas's headline/Featured section consistent with those topics, and avoid drifting into unrelated life-lesson posts. Evidence: verified-source (mechanism) + practitioner-consensus (topic share) + inference (keywording). [LinkedIn Engineering 2026-03-12; Creator Science #307 2026-06-02]
2. **Every post needs at least one detail only Thomas can know.** LinkedIn (2026-09-15) reports views of content classified as AI slop reduced by 40%, an "AI slop" feedback button used by 1M+ members in two weeks after its August launch, and (2026-06-04) a 94%-accuracy classifier for generic content that limits distribution beyond the immediate network. Rule: one dated observation, decision, screenshot, user message (with consent) or number with its definition per post; delete any sentence that could appear on any founder's profile. Evidence: verified-source. [news.linkedin.com 2026-09-15, 2026-06-04]
3. **No keyword-comment CTAs ("Kommentiere BRIEF und ich schick dir..."), no pods, no comment automation.** LinkedIn names "comment to agree prompts or videos that don't match the text", comment automation and pods as demoted; its Community Policies forbid artificially increasing engagement; the User Agreement 8.2 forbids bots that comment or send messages. Practitioners report keyword CTAs are now classified as bait; the circulating workaround (put the CTA inside the image) is evasion and is not adopted. Lead CTA instead: one plain sentence ("Wenn ihr als Verein so etwas testen wollt: schreibt mir kurz.") or a direct link. Evidence: verified-source + practitioner-consensus. [ImprovingTheFeed 2026-03-12; Professional Community Policies; User Agreement 2025-11-03; linkedinpreview 2026]
4. **Expect lower reach; measure conversations, not impressions; post 2-3 times a week.** Van der Blom 2025 (1.8M posts): views -50%, engagement -25%, follower growth -59% YoY; 2026 report (1.3M posts): reach -60% for active creators over two years, while engagement per impression improved; recommended frequency now 2-4 posts/week, filler (quotes, memes) hurts. Rule for Thomas: 2 posts/week sustainable baseline (e.g. Tue + Thu), 3 when there is real material; never a filler post. Evidence: practitioner data (self-published, not peer reviewed). [Creator Science 2026-06-02; Agorapulse 2025-09-03]
5. **Default format: text + one real image; carousel (PDF) for explainers; video as a deliberate test only; skip polls.** Van der Blom 2026 (3-posts/week plan: text+image, carousel, text+image) avoids polls, video and text-only unless you are an exceptional writer (reviewer correction: he does not endorse video "if you enjoy it"). Because Thomas will produce Instagram Reels anyway, re-using one Reel natively on LinkedIn is a cheap test, but only if the post text matches the video: LinkedIn explicitly demotes "videos that don't match the text" (ImprovingTheFeed 2026-03-12). Record it as an experiment, not a default. AuthoredUp (updated 2026-08-11) reports multipliers documents 1.45x, images 1.18x, video 1.10x, text 0.88x but polls 1.64x - the poll data conflict, so polls stay optional. Best image for BnB: a real photo (handwritten letter, envelope, Briefkasten, desk in Bremen) or an honest screenshot - not a stock or AI image. Evidence: practitioner data, conflicting on polls. [Creator Science; AuthoredUp]
6. **Comments on other people's posts are the main distribution and warm-up channel.** Van der Blom: 25-30 min/day in total, of which ~15 min go to replying under his own posts and ~15 min to 5-10 external comments (reviewer correction: the 25-30 min is not all outbound commenting). The "curated list of 12-20 people" and "most early comments are AI-written" claims could not be found in the cited interview: downgraded to unverified. Well Met (vendor, B2B-buyer framing, no data source) recommends 3-5 comments over 7-14 days before connecting with mid-level people; the earlier "2-3 comments over 1-2 weeks raise reply rates" figure was a misquote and has no evidence behind it. LinkedIn (2026-06-04) limits distribution of "responses that simply restate the original post". Rule for a solo founder: minimum 10-15 min/day, 3-5 substantive comments on posts of the people on Thomas's priority list (journalists, NGO staff, creators, funders he actually wants to reach), plus replying under his own posts on posting days. Each comment adds a fact, a counter-example, or a question from Thomas's experience; no link; no product mention unless directly asked. Evidence: verified-source (anti-restate) + practitioner (single interview) + inference (time budget). [Creator Science; news.linkedin.com 2026-06-04; getwellmet 2026]
7. **The hook must land in ~140 characters / ~3 lines on mobile - German needs to be shorter than English.** Postformatter's own measurements (LinkedIn publishes no number) give ~140 characters / ~3 lines on mobile and ~210 characters on desktop (reviewer correction: 210 is desktop, not mobile); line breaks count and the numbers drift with layout tests. German compounds eat the budget. Rule: line 1 <= ~70 characters, lines 1+2 <= ~140; the promise is complete before the cut; check the actual composer preview on the phone. Evidence: practitioner-consensus (no official spec). [postformatter; goextrovert 2026]
8. **External links: conflicting data, so decide per goal.** Reports: van der Blom (as cited secondhand) one link -18.8% median reach; AuthoredUp: single-link posts worst, preview card removal helps; Saywhat Q1 2026 (~400k posts): posts with links did better, attributed to resource-rich content; links in comments reportedly also suppressed. LinkedIn has published no link penalty. Rule: reach/awareness post -> no link, name "brief-nach-berlin.de" as plain text or point to the Featured section; action post (campaign launch, call for NGOs) -> one link at the end and accept the cost. Record which variant was used. Evidence: practitioner data, conflicting. [AuthoredUp; feedboss/Saywhat summary 2026]
9. **Be available for 60 minutes after posting and answer every substantive comment with a real reply.** Replies-to-replies create conversation depth; practitioners report strong early-reply effects (e.g. "+64% comments" claims, unverified). LinkedIn's ranker refreshes "within minutes" of new engagement, which makes early genuine discussion plausible as a signal. Rule: post only when Thomas can stay for an hour; ask one answerable question; reply with substance, not "Danke!". Evidence: verified-source (near-real-time refresh) + practitioner claims. [LinkedIn Engineering 2026-03-12]
10. **Timing in Germany: low priority; pick a slot when Thomas can stay 60 minutes, default Tue-Thu morning.** (Reviewer downgrade: vendor aggregate data, small effect compared with topic, hook and the 60-minute reply window. Do not let slot testing become a project.) Aggregated vendor studies for Germany converge on Tue-Thu mornings to early afternoon (Metricool/Shopify 2026: Wed/Thu 9-13). Journalists and NGO staff read in the morning; civic-minded private users may read evenings - unknown. Evidence: vendor observational + inference. [Shopify DE 2026; Buffer 2026-09-09]
11. **Optimise for saves and sends, not likes.** LinkedIn analytics now show saves and "sends on LinkedIn" per post (official). A send is the clearest "I forwarded this to someone" signal - exactly the organic-recommendation behaviour BnB needs. Explainers ("Wer ist zuständig?") are save/send material. Evidence: verified-source (metrics exist) + inference. [LinkedIn Help a6538517]
12. **A LinkedIn newsletter is a one-shot network invite - use it deliberately later.** LinkedIn sends all connections and followers an invitation when the first edition is published; subscribers then get notification + email per edition. Van der Blom reports newsletters far outperform standalone articles and that his weekly newsletter drives ~80% of his conversions to paying customers (his own business, self-reported; the "articles ~0.4x" figure was not found in the cited interview and is unverified). Rule: only start once 4+ editions of real material exist (e.g. monthly "Briefe aus Bremen: was ich beim Bauen lerne"). Evidence: verified-source (invite mechanics) + practitioner data. [LinkedIn Help a522525; Creator Science]
13. **Pick the emotional centre before the hook (repo feedback 2026-09-18).** Thomas rejected a hook built on the traffic spike; the meaning was "people recommend it without me". Rule: before drafting, state privately whether the post is about the metric, the mechanism, or what it means to Thomas, and offer two openings from different centres. Evidence: founder feedback. (Reviewer note: `workspace/voice.md` rejects "two variants plus metadata" for outreach messages. For posts, give one full draft plus one alternative opening line only, not two full posts.)

## Added by review (2026-10-04): three points that most affect leads

14. **The post-to-DM bridge hits free-account limits: plan for them.** LinkedIn Help (checked 2026-10-04): basic members can add a personalized note to only **3 connection requests per month**, max 200 characters; Premium is unlimited. Messaging people who are not 1st-degree connections generally needs InMail [PRUEFEN: Thomas's account type and whether the target has an open profile]. Consequence: a commenter Thomas is not connected with cannot simply be DMed. Rule: (a) reply publicly first, (b) send a note-free connection request when the public exchange makes the context obvious, (c) spend the 3 monthly notes only on top-priority multipliers (journalist, funder, large NGO), (d) DM after acceptance, with the comment as the reason. Evidence: verified-source. [https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153]
15. **Thomas's profile is the landing page for every post: fix it before increasing volume.** LinkedIn's ranking uses identity signals ("your profile details such as location, workplace, and skills") together with content and activity signals, and post analytics report profile views from a post. Someone curious after a post looks at the headline and Featured section, not at the website. Rule: the headline names BnB and what it does in plain German (e.g. "Ich baue Brief nach Berlin: kostenlos einen persönlichen Brief an die zuständige Stelle schreiben | Civic Tech, Bremen"); Featured holds (1) brief-nach-berlin.de, (2) the campaign/NGO page or a one-pager for Vereine [PRUEFEN: live], (3) the best explainer post; About ends with one sentence on who should write to Thomas and about what. This also gives reach posts a link-free CTA ("Link steht oben in meinem Profil"). Evidence: verified-source (identity signal, profile-view metric) + inference (landing-page effect). [https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724; https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6538517]
16. **Make partners part of the post instead of only pitching them afterwards.** LinkedIn lists "tagged people, companies, or topics" as a content signal in feed ranking, and a mention notifies the person. The highest-yield lead post for BnB is not "ich suche Vereine" but a post *about* one real Verein, creator or letter-writer (with consent) who did something with the tool, mentioning them, so they repost it to their own audience: the exact multiplier audience Thomas wants. Rule: at most 1-2 mentions per post, only of people/organisations actually in the post and who agreed; ask them before posting ("Darf ich euch erwähnen?"), never tag for reach; after posting, a one-line DM: "Ist online, falls ihr es teilen wollt." Evidence: verified-source (tag signal) + inference (partner reach); no published effect size. [https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724]

# 2. Post archetypes for BnB (replaces sub-personas)

Each archetype: purpose / who it attracts / lead it can create / CTA / one German hook. All hooks use only recorded facts; anything else is marked.

1. **Organische Weiterempfehlung** - meaning over metric. Attracts multipliers, media. Lead: journalist or creator asks "wie kam das?". CTA: none or "Falls du es weitergegeben hast: Wie hast du es erklärt?" (reviewer: asking "wem" invites people to name third parties; asking how they explained it is easier to answer and gives Thomas wording). Hook: "Ich habe diese Woche nichts gepostet. Trotzdem kamen Briefe aus Orten, in denen ich niemanden kenne. [PRUEFEN: stimmt "nichts gepostet"? Zeitraum + Orte aus Analytics; keine Kleinstädte nennen, die auf Personen schließen lassen]"
2. **Zuständigkeits-Erklärer (Carousel, save-/send-Material)** - Bund vs. Land vs. Kommune with everyday examples. Attracts teachers, civic educators, NGOs. Lead: request to use it in a workshop. CTA: "Speicher dir das für den nächsten Ärger." Hook: "Kaputter Radweg, fehlende Kita-Plätze, Bahnchaos. Drei Probleme, oft drei verschiedene Adressen. [PRUEFEN: Zuordnung je Beispiel]"
3. **Bewusste Produktentscheidung** - why no automatic sending, why handwritten. Attracts NGOs wary of mass mail, funders. Lead: campaign conversation. CTA: question. Hook: "Brief nach Berlin verschickt keine Briefe für dich. Das war eine bewusste Entscheidung. [PRUEFEN: Thomas' echter Grund in einem Satz, z. B. Handschrift wird anders gelesen als Massenmails]" (reviewer: the original "Ich könnte ... automatisch verschicken" asserts an unbuilt capability and uses the "Ich könnte X. Ich mache es nicht." template that reads as AI hook.)
4. **Fehler und Fix (build in public)** - one real bug or wrong recipient and what changed. Attracts tech/civic-tech peers, funders (shows rigor). Lead: expert feedback, intro. Hook: "[PRUEFEN: realer Fehler] Mein Tool hat jemandem die falsche Stelle vorgeschlagen. Das habe ich daraus gebaut:"
5. **Stimme einer Nutzerin / eines Nutzers** - one review or message, with explicit consent. Attracts general audience, media. Lead: journalist wants a protagonist. Hook: "„[ZITAT von der Stimmen-Seite]“ [PRUEFEN: Einwilligung zur Nutzung auf LinkedIn] Diese Nachricht kam am [DATUM]."
6. **Zahl ehrlich eingeordnet** - counter with its definition. Attracts media, funders. Lead: data request, funding chat. Hook: "3.023 - so viele Briefe zeigt der Zähler auf brief-nach-berlin.de heute. Was die Zahl nicht sagt: [PRUEFEN: generiert vs. abgeschickt]"
7. **Begründete Position** - labelled opinion on civic participation. Attracts debate, podcasters. Lead: podcast/panel invite. Hook: "Eine Petition unterschreibe ich in zehn Sekunden. Für einen eigenen Brief brauche ich länger [PRUEFEN: echte Dauer aus eigener Nutzung]. Ich glaube, genau deshalb wird er anders gelesen. [PRUEFEN: als Meinung kennzeichnen, keine Wirkung behaupten]" (reviewer: framed as Thomas's own experience and opinion; the original implied a measured duration and an effect on recipients.)
8. **Aktueller Anlass** - news moment -> who is responsible -> next step. Attracts people angry about the topic, creators covering it. Lead: creator shares a prefilled link. Hook: "Seit [ANLASS] fragen mich Leute: Wem schreibe ich dazu eigentlich? Kurze Antwort: [ZUSTÄNDIGE EBENE, PRUEFEN]"
9. **Einladung an Vereine/Initiativen** - campaign flow announcement. Attracts NGO staff. Lead: campaign setup call. CTA + link once. Hook: "Für Vereine und Initiativen: Eure Leute können mit einem Link einen eigenen Brief an die zuständige Stelle schreiben. [PRUEFEN: Kampagnenseite live]"
10. **Konkrete Bitte (Solo-Gründer)** - "Ich suche drei..." Attracts helpers, funders, intros. Lead: intro or pilot. Hook: "Ich suche drei Vereine oder Initiativen, die im [Monat] eine Briefaktion zu ihrem Thema ausprobieren. Aufwand für euch: [PRUEFEN: realistisch, z. B. ein Link an eure Leute]. Kostenlos." (reviewer: who/what/effort in the preview; "Ich baue ... allein" moves to line 3, it is context, not the hook.)

Rotation: alternate a reach archetype (1, 2, 7, 8) with a lead archetype (6, 9, 10); never two asks in a row.

## Pre-publish checklist

1. Emotional centre chosen (metric / mechanism / meaning) and hook built from it.
2. Hook complete within ~140 characters; phone preview checked.
3. Topic words visible in lines 1-3; fits Thomas's 2-3 topics.
4. At least one founder-only detail; every number dated and defined; `[PRUEFEN]` resolved or removed.
5. One point; body delivers the hook's promise; ending returns to it.
6. Zero or one CTA (PS counts); no keyword-comment, no "Agree?".
7. Link decision recorded (none / one at end / Featured).
8. Real image or screenshot, personal data and towns of individuals blurred; consent for any quote.
9. "ich", not "wir"; non-partisan wording; no implied political effect.
10. Thomas can stay 60 minutes to reply; slot Tue-Thu morning noted.
11. 3 relevant people to notify personally only if they would genuinely want it (no mass tagging; tag only people mentioned).
12. Goal metric for this post set: replies from target group, sends, DMs, campaign inquiries.

## Post-to-DM bridge playbook

Signal strength (strongest first): asks a question or describes own case in a comment > reposts with own words > comments substantively > sends/saves (not visible per person) > likes > profile view (weak; never mention it).
1. Reply publicly first, with substance, within the hour.
2. DM within 24-48 h only if the comment shows a concrete need, role fit (NGO, creator, journalist, funder) or explicit interest. Never DM all likers.
3. The DM quotes or paraphrases their comment, adds one thing that did not fit publicly, and asks one small question or offers one concrete thing.
4. Connection request (if not connected): note-free by default, because free accounts get only 3 personalized notes per month (finding 14); use a note (max 200 characters, referencing the thread, no pitch) only for top-priority multipliers. DM after acceptance.
5. Log the contact in `workspace/` with the post reference, so a later post-led conversation is traceable to the post (lead attribution).
6. Outbound warm-up: for a priority multiplier, 2-3 substantive comments on their posts over 1-2 weeks (via `warm-contact-comment`), then a DM that references the exchange - never "Ich habe deine Posts kommentiert".

# 4. Follow-up cadence (post-originated conversations)

- Max 2 follow-ups after the first DM. Spacing: day 5-7, then day 14-21. Then stop and log `no_reply`.
- Each follow-up adds something new: a new post that answers their comment's topic, a concrete campaign example, an updated number with definition, an intro offer, or a direct answer to a likely objection (Datenschutz, Neutralität). Never "wollte nur nachhaken".
- Public-first re-engagement: a genuine comment on their next relevant post can replace the first follow-up.
- Channel switch to email only for journalists/funders with a published business address and an editorial or funding reason (see section 6); one email, subject as truthful headline.
- After a "nicht jetzt": ask once for a better time or person; next contact only with a new reason (new feature, news moment) after 2-3 months.

# 6. Legal / platform / ethics risks (risk guidance, not legal advice)

- **LinkedIn rules:** User Agreement 8.2 forbids bots that comment, like, message; scraping and browser extensions. Community Policies forbid artificial engagement, pre-agreed like/share swaps, untargeted promotional messages and promotional invitations. "Comment X and I DM you" via automation tools violates both.
- **UWG § 7:** email advertising without prior consent is "unzumutbare Belästigung" (§ 7 Abs. 2 Nr. 2); donation or campaign-partner asks may count as commercial communication via WE AID - treat cold email cautiously, prefer LinkedIn DMs within an existing exchange or published press/funding contacts with a clear editorial/funding reason.
- **DSGVO Art. 9:** letters and issues reveal political opinions (special category). Never post screenshots of letters, emails or analytics showing individuals or small towns linkable to a person; quotes only with explicit, documented consent.
- **Werbekennzeichnung / Pressekodex:** if a creator or partner posts about BnB in exchange for anything (money, reach swap, donation), it needs labelling; for journalists, offer material, never ask for favourable coverage (Pressekodex Ziffer 7 separation).
- **Impressum (§ 5 DDG):** business-like social profiles may need a reachable imprint link; check that the LinkedIn profile/Featured links to one.
- **Neutrality:** non-partisan brand - hooks about current events must not take party positions or imply that letters change outcomes.
