# Delta: warm outreach and re-engagement after silence

Scope: what is new against `research/2026-10-02-follow-up-evidence.md`, `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, `research/2026-10-04-call-prep-multipliers.md`, `knowledge/strategies/follow-up.md` and `knowledge/channels/personal-network.md`. Those files rest on sales-email vendor data and practitioner advice. They contain no peer-reviewed evidence on people the founder already knows. This note adds it, and says where it stops.

"Read as of" says what was actually opened on 2026-11-11. Paywalled journal pages were not opened; abstracts came from Europe PMC or NCBI records. Effect sizes beyond the abstract are therefore not claimed. No outreach was made and nothing from `workspace/` was used.

## Contradicts or weakens existing guidance

- `knowledge/strategies/follow-up.md`, Touch plan table, LinkedIn DM row ("Re-entry only with a new trigger after 60+ days, at most once a quarter") and `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md` section 2 ("Re-engagement (>=60-90 days, only with a new trigger)"): weakened for people Thomas already knows or has met. Claims 1-3 show that a short no-ask contact from an acquaintance is received better than the sender expects and that the sender's hesitation is the bigger failure. The trigger-and-quarterly rule rests on practitioner advice for strangers and has no source; it stays reasonable for asks and for people who never replied (claim 10 shows no benchmark either way). Limit: the studies test no-ask check-ins with acquaintances, not messages that carry an ask, and not German business contacts.
- `knowledge/channels/personal-network.md`, "Ask for a referral only after the context is clear and the request is easy to decline": not contradicted. It gains a concrete method (claim 8), and a boundary: the "people will help more than you fear" evidence (claim 4) comes from face-to-face and scenario tasks, and email shows the reverse (claim 5). Do not read claim 4 as a promise that a DM intro ask succeeds.
- `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, finding 5 ("Message existing connections first") and finding 8 (warm intros): the vendor-data reasoning there is untouched, but nothing in the weak-tie literature supports it directly (claim 6 is about jobs). Do not cite Rajkumar et al. as evidence for LinkedIn reply rates.
- `knowledge/channels/personal-network.md`, Intro kit item 1 ("when later, add one short apology clause"): unsupported either way. No study of acknowledging a gap versus ignoring it was found (see below); it remains a judgment call.

## Proposed patches

- `knowledge/channels/personal-network.md` -> add a short section "Reconnecting with someone you know": a one- or two-line message with no ask is welcome more often than he expects; do not let imagined awkwardness cancel it; no fake warmth stays -> claims 1, 2, 3.
- `knowledge/strategies/follow-up.md` -> in the LinkedIn re-entry cell, split "known or met contact: a no-ask check-in is allowed without a new trigger, once" from "no prior reply or cold: new trigger after 60+ days"; mark the quarterly cap as judgment -> claims 1, 3, 10.
- `knowledge/channels/personal-network.md` (Intro kit item 7 and 5) -> note that the referral question works best live (call, voice, video); a text ask is a weaker channel, so offer the call or voice note first when the person is known -> claims 4, 5.
- `knowledge/channels/personal-network.md` (intro request template) -> add one ready-made decline sentence, for example "Wenn es gerade nicht passt, sag einfach 'lieber nicht', das ist völlig okay." -> claim 8.
- `knowledge/strategies/follow-up.md` (or the event-contact guidance if one exists) -> mark every day count for conference follow-up as "practice, not research"; keep the repo's rule to name the specific conversation in line one -> claim 9.
- `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md` -> add a pointer line under Re-engagement: "Peer-reviewed support exists only for no-ask contact with acquaintances (see 2026-11-11 delta); no re-engagement benchmark found" -> claims 1, 10.

## Not found or unverified

- **Acknowledging the gap versus ignoring it:** no study found that tests either message form. Aknin and Sandstrom name guilt and awkwardness as barriers but do not test wording. The repo's apology-clause and gap-acknowledging advice stays opinion.
- **What a good message after 30+ days of silence contains:** no controlled evidence. Press articles say "personal, shared memory, no immediate ask"; these are secondary and were not traced to a study.
- **Conference follow-up timing and content:** only blogs and vendor templates; no study (claim 9).
- **Asking for an introduction:** no peer-reviewed field experiment on intro-request acceptance found. Double opt-in and forwardable blurb remain practitioner consensus, as in the 2026-10-04 notes.
- **Full texts:** Liu et al., Zhao and Epley (published version), Roghanizad and Bohns, Schlund et al., Aknin and Sandstrom were read at abstract level or from the authors' manuscript only; paywalled or blocked pages (APA PsycNet, ScienceDirect, SAGE, Science, PubMed web pages) could not be opened. Numbers beyond the abstract are not used.
- **Not opened at all:** Walter, Levin and Murnighan (2015) "Reconnection Choices" (Organization Science 26(5)): only a search summary (people prefer contacts they spent most time with, though novel and engaged contacts are more valuable). Brooks, Gino and Schweitzer (2015) on seeking advice and Huang et al. (2017) on question-asking and liking: known by search summary only. All unverified.
- **Germany:** every primary study above uses non-German samples. No German-language replication or DACH data found for any claim.
- **Dean Jackson's "9-word email"** (already cited in the 2026-10-04 note): still unverified; no original source retrieved.
