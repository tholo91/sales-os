# Bottom line

The repo already prevents spam. What it lacks is the part that gets replies: a sharp trigger in line one, a give-first asset that costs the recipient nothing, one interest question, and 2 value-adding follow-ups instead of 1 nudge and silence. The best "free micro-gift" depends on the persona (review correction): a **sample letter on their topic** for creators, an **unlisted prefilled campaign page** for NGOs, a **checked local data point** for media, and nothing unsolicited for funders. It shows the product without a pitch and lets the person answer "ja, zeig mal". The step most often missing is what comes after the "ja": a share kit within 24 hours (finding 13). A reply only becomes a lead there.

# 1. Findings that change drafting (ranked)

1. **Relevance beats personalization. Line one is the trigger, not a compliment.** Practitioners agree that the opener should reference something the person *authored or engaged with*, tied to the problem you solve. Becc Holland ranks content they wrote, content they engaged with, and self-described traits as the top-responding premises. Kyle Coleman calls hobby or dog-breed personalization off-putting and asks for "relevance in the first line". Sam McKenna's SMYKM approach uses facts that only make sense to this one reader. Rule: the first sentence names one dated public action of theirs (post, campaign, Reel, article, Plenum) **and** says why it touches letters to politics. If the sentence would still work for someone else, rewrite it. *practitioner-consensus; the three practitioner sources are secondary summaries and were not re-checked in review.* [Holland via Signum](https://medium.com/signum-ai/how-to-build-b2b-outbound-sales-and-do-personalization-at-scale-sales-playbook-from-becc-holland-e00b74e003e7), [Coleman podcast](https://www.everand.com/podcast/589196868/47-How-to-personalize-your-outbound-email-at-scale-Kyle-Coleman-VP-Revenue-Clari-Kyle-Coleman-returns-to-the-show-for-round-2-of-email-prospect), [SMYKM summary](https://outplay.ai/blog/show-me-you-know-me)
2. **Short and simple wins. Pitching costs replies.** In Gong's 28M+ cold emails, emails of 3–4 sentences and 100 words or fewer got the highest reply rates (checked in review 2026-10-04). Gong also reports that pitching cuts replies by up to 57%. Lavender recommends 25–50 words (under 75) and reports 67% more replies at a grade 3–5 reading level. Instantly reports that top performers average under 80 words. Rule for BnB: cold first touch **40–90 words** (Thomas's confirmed DMs carry a 1-sentence project explanation, so 90 is the ceiling, not the target). Use short sentences, no subordinate-clause chains and no "innovative civic-tech solution". After a reply, a longer message is fine. *verified-source (vendor data, observational)*. [Gong 2025/2026](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says), [Lavender Cold Email 101](https://lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-101), [Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
3. **Ask for interest, not time. A permission-to-give question is one good option, not the only one.** Gong's 304k CTA analysis favoring interest CTAs over meeting asks is already cited in the repo; the review could not re-open that page (404 on the guessed URL), so treat it as *not re-verified*. Lavender (checked) recommends a "call to conversation" instead of a meeting ask. Josh Braun (checked) advises against proposing call times, but his recommended CTAs are thought-provoking questions about the prospect's situation, not permission-to-send questions. Justin Welsh's first touch (checked, 2024-01-20) asks for **nothing** and only gives value. So no source directly tests "Soll ich dir X schicken?"; it is an *inference* that combines interest CTA and give-first. Rule: end with **one easy question**: either permission to send a concrete asset ("Soll ich dir den Entwurf schicken?") or a short question about their situation ("Fragen eure Leute nach so einem nächsten Schritt?"). Ask for a call only when the person has already engaged or when it is the explicitly desired goal with a strong trigger (as the repo and Thomas's 2026-10-02 voice preference already allow). *vendor data + practitioner advice; the exact CTA form is inference*. [Braun CTAs](https://joshbraun.com/cold-email-ctas/), [Welsh cold DM](https://www.justinwelsh.me/newsletter/how-to-write-a-successful-cold-dm)
4. **Follow-ups produce a large share of replies, but the curve flattens after touch 3.** Instantly (2025 data): 58% of replies come from step 1 and 42% from follow-ups. Recommended spacing there is 3–4 days, with a 4–7 step range. Belkins (7.5M emails over 12 months, page updated 2026-06-26, checked in review): steps 2–6 bring 58.6% of replies, step 3 alone books 35.6% of email-sourced meetings, and returns diminish beyond step 5. ~~Rising unsubscribe and spam complaints from about the 4th email~~ **removed in review**: the Belkins primary page contains no such figure, and the claim came only from third-party summaries. Both datasets are B2B email *sequences* with 4–7 steps to strangers. Instantly even says fewer than four touches "gives up too early". A personal civic DM to a named multiplier is a different situation, so the touch count is a judgment call, not a data result. Rule for a solo civic founder: **3 touches total per person per channel (first touch + 2 value follow-ups), then park.** The repo's "max one unanswered nudge" probably leaves reply potential unused. *vendor data (verified numbers); the transfer to BnB and the count of 3 are inference*. [Belkins follow-up 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics), [Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
5. **Message existing connections first. Connection notes are scarce on free accounts.** LinkedIn's help page (checked 2026-10-04, "last updated 5 months ago") says basic members can add a personalized note to **3 connection requests per month**, max 200 characters; an older help page still says five. Premium removes the cap. Belkins/Expandi 2025 data (15M touchpoints, checked in review): a note slightly lowers acceptance (25.3% vs 27.6%). The reply lift among acceptors (8.18% vs 5.28%) appears on the Belkins *follow-up statistics* page, not on the LinkedIn study page. Messenger campaigns to already-connected contacts had a 12.2% total reply rate. Rule: (a) start with 1st-degree connections who show a fitting signal; (b) for new people, comment publicly first and then send a blank request, so the name is already known; (c) save the 3 notes for top targets. *verified-source (LinkedIn) + vendor data*. [LinkedIn help a563153](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153), [Belkins LinkedIn study](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study)
6. **Give-first assets beat explanation.** Practitioners repeatedly use a small custom resource as the first or second touch (Welsh: touch 1 = useful value, touch 2 = custom resource tied to what they just discussed). Vendor claims for personal video (Vidyard, Loom: "3–5x" replies) are marketing, so treat them as a direction, not a number. BnB has three native gifts: (a) a sample letter on their topic, (b) a 45–60s screen recording that enters their topic into BnB, (c) a prefilled campaign-page mock for NGOs. Make clear that nothing was published in their name. *practitioner-consensus; vendor numbers unverified*. [Vidyard tools page](https://www.vidyard.com/blog/prospecting-tools/)
7. **Voice notes are a follow-up tool, not an opener.** LinkedIn voice messages are mobile-only, go only to 1st-degree connections, and last up to 60 seconds (official). Practitioners advise using them after a text touch or reply, or to revive a stalled thread, not cold. The "30–40% lift" claims are unsourced. Rule: use one ≤45s voice note as follow-up 2 for warm-ish 1st-degree contacts. It shows Thomas is a real person, which suits "ich, nicht wir". *verified-source (feature) + practitioner-consensus*. [LinkedIn voice messaging](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/member/product/voice-messaging-on-linkedin-giving-you-more-ways-to-have-conversations), [Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-voice-messages-when-and-how-to-use-for-sales/)
8. **Warm intros via double opt-in and a forwardable blurb are the highest-leverage ask Thomas can make.** In the standard VC/B2B pattern, the connector checks with the target first. The requester makes this effortless with a 3–4 sentence blurb written about himself in the third person, which the connector can forward unchanged. The intros a connector contact offered in an advisory call (evidence 2026-10-01) fit this pattern, but they were *conditional* (e.g., one intro only "if direct outreach fails"). The intro request must respect that condition and must not claim a firmer promise. *practitioner-consensus (sources not re-checked in review)*. [Double opt-in explainer](https://getboomerang.ai/glossaries/double-opt-in-introductions), [Forwardable email](https://medium.com/@constancedeng/the-art-of-the-forwardable-email-a-template-71fb26c1d7d3)
9. **Email subject: short and specific, except for press.** Lavender (checked): "boring is best", 1–3 words that read like an internal note. The Gong article (checked) says to avoid numbers and questions in subjects. ~~"free"/"demo" lower opens by up to 17.9%~~ **removed in review**: the figure was not on the cited Gong page. The exception is journalists: the subject is a candidate headline (advice from a journalist contact, recorded in the workspace). Partner email subjects: `Briefe zu [Thema]`, `[Kampagne] + Briefe`. *vendor data*. [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says), [Lavender](https://lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-101)
10. **German register: mirror, don't guess.** German LinkedIn is mostly "du". Knigge-style advice still recommends "Sie" for strangers and letting the other person offer "du". Rule: mirror the register in their own posts, bio or replies. If there is no visible signal: NGO, creator and startup contexts → "du"; ministries, foundation boards, public broadcasters, senior editors → "Sie". (Review: removed the "under ~45" age heuristic. It is unreliable and conflicts with `workspace/voice.md`, which says not to infer du/Sie from crude proxies.) "Moin" is an honest Bremen identity marker and works for both. *practitioner-consensus*. [gap-c Knigge](https://www.gap-c.de/duzen-oder-siezen-die-ungeschriebenen-regeln-fur-die-korrekte-ansprache-im-business-netzwerk/), [LinkedIn News DE](https://www.linkedin.com/news/story/was-f%C3%BCrs-siezen-und-was-f%C3%BCrs-duzen-spricht-5810268/)
11. **Small batches beat volume.** Woodpecker (summary): campaigns under 50 recipients 5.8% vs 2.1% at 1,000+. Belkins: lower volume, higher reply rates. This fits the 10–15-contact batches a journalist contact recommended. *vendor data, observational; not re-checked in review. Small batches correlate with more personalization, so this does not show that volume itself hurts.* [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)

### Added in review: three points that matter most for multiplier replies and leads

12. **Time the message to their action moment and frame it around their people, not the tool.** A multiplier says yes when BnB solves *their* problem at *their* moment: the hearing, Aktionstag or vote they are mobilizing for, or the Reel whose comments ask "und was kann ich jetzt tun?" BIT's EAST framework, a public-policy behavior-change framework (2014, updated 2024), names *Timely*: prompt people when they are most receptive. It also names *Attractive*: capture attention through framing that is relevant to them. Gong (checked) shows pitching cuts replies by up to 57%. Rule: write within a few days of the signal. Line two says what *their supporters, viewers or readers* get (a concrete next step), not how BnB works. If there is no upcoming moment, use the latest post as the trigger and say so honestly. *verified framework + vendor data; the transfer is inference.* [BIT EAST](https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/), [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says)
13. **After a "ja", the lead is won or lost in the next 24 hours: hand over a ready-to-use share kit.** A reply is not a lead. The commitment happens when sharing costs the multiplier almost nothing. EAST *Easy* says reducing hassle factors and effort raises action. Default kit: (a) a link with their topic prefilled, (b) a 2-sentence caption in their register, (c) one visual or a 30–45s screen recording, (d) a date suggestion tied to their action moment. Then close the loop: after their share, send one line with what happened (only real numbers, e.g., [PRUEFEN: Kampagnenstatistik der Seite verfügbar?]) and thank them. A loop closed with real outcomes makes the next ask easier. That last point is *inference*: there is no source for repeat-partner rates. [BIT EAST](https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/)
14. **Use social proof, but only real proof, plus a referral fallback for organizations.** EAST *Social*: people orient on what comparable others do. For an unknown solo founder, one true peer signal raises trust more than any adjective. Examples: "[PRUEFEN: Lage der Nation hat das Tool erwähnt]", one quote from the public /stimmen page, or a named NGO that ran a campaign page (only with its consent). Never imply a partnership that does not exist. For organizations (NGOs, editorial teams, foundations), the first contact is often not the decision-maker. Thomas's confirmed-native LinkedIn samples (`workspace/voice.md`, 2026-08-21) end with a low-pressure "falls du nicht die richtige Person bist, leite gern weiter". This is a fallback inside the same ask, not a second ask. *verified framework + confirmed founder voice; effect size unknown.* [BIT EAST](https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/)

## What the repo gets wrong or over-hedges (inference)

- **"One unanswered nudge max"** (`follow-up.md`) is a relationship guardrail without evidence. Replace it with: up to 2 follow-ups that each bring *new* value, spaced 4–10 working days apart, then a short park message; stop at any "no". Keep the stricter rule for cold email in Germany (see section 6).
- **"A first message may end without an ask"**: *downgraded in review.* The researcher's own source (Welsh) recommends a no-ask first touch, and Thomas's 2026-10-02 coaching preference says to omit a question "when it would be manufactured". Better rule: for a private multiplier DM, default to one natural, easy question. A no-ask message is fine for public comments, pure thank-yous and warming touches. That a no-ask DM "usually ends in a polite like" is an untested hunch.
- **"Do not switch channels"**: fine against circumvention, but too broad. An expected route (press mailbox, NGO contact form, foundation application portal) after LinkedIn silence is legitimate if it brings a new angle and there was no "no".
- **Evidence prose is inside drafting files.** Skills need a 10-line checklist plus examples. The caveats belong in research/.
- **Missing pieces:** a give-first asset step, a warm-intro blurb template, a re-engagement rule, a weekly volume target, a "lead = dated commitment" definition and (added in review) a post-"ja" share kit and close-the-loop step.

# 2. Playbooks by message type (with persona overlays)

**Cold LinkedIn DM (after connection or to an existing 1st-degree contact).** Flooded with: pitch-slaps right after accept, AI-written flattery. Values: being seen for one specific thing, zero work. Reply trigger: a precise signal + an asset already made for them + a yes/no question. Kills it: product biography, "wir", several asks, calendar link, "ich wollte mich mal vorstellen". Structure: signal (1 sentence) → what *their* people get, with BnB in plain words (1–2 sentences) → give-first offer → one question (+ the referral fallback for organizations, finding 14). Smallest ask: "Soll ich dir den Entwurf schicken?" Lead = they say yes, or they name their own use case.

**Connection note (≤200 characters, only 3/month on free accounts).** Use it only for top targets without a prior public interaction. Signal + who Thomas is + no ask beyond connecting. Without a note: comment publicly 1–7 days before and send the request blank.

**Email (journalist pitch; partner email only via an expected route).** Journalists are flooded with press releases and get no local angle. They value a verifiable news angle, numbers with a definition, and a reachable founder. Structure per `editorial-pitch.md`: headline subject → why this person → news + proof → one founder sentence → "Interesse an einem kurzen Gespräch zur Geschichte?" Lead = a call booked or a fact-check request.

**Instagram DM (creators).** Flooded with brand deals and requests from non-followers. DMs from non-followers land in Requests; whether they notify is a vendor claim (Inro), not verified. Practitioner advice: follow, leave 2–3 real comments over a few days, then DM; 2–3 short blocks; honor "email only" in the bio. Do not use the paid "partnership messages" path for an unpaid ask. Lead = they test it on camera, or agree to look at a sample letter.

**Warm-intro request.** Make it effortless: one ask, a named target, and a reason why the target benefits. Include a forwardable blurb and offer an out ("Wenn es nicht passt, sag einfach Bescheid"). Lead = the connector confirms they forwarded it, or a double opt-in intro email arrives.

**Follow-up 1 (+4–6 working days).** Brings the asset itself, or a new local data point. Never "wollte nochmal nachhaken". Ask the same question in a smaller form.

**Follow-up 2 (+7–10 working days later).** New angle: a different benefit, a voice note (1st-degree), or a relevant news hook. One sentence of context, one question.

**Park / breakup (around day 21).** One or two sentences: the thread rests, the door stays open, and the asset stays available. No guilt and no "Soll ich aufhören?" gimmick (practitioners report that it works, but it feels manipulative in a civic context).

**Re-engagement (≥60–90 days, only with a new trigger).** Practitioners: revive with a *reason* (their new post, a BnB milestone, an election or news hook), not on a calendar. A short one-question message is a common practitioner pattern (Dean Jackson's "9-word email"; not verified in review): "Ist [Thema] bei euch noch aktuell?".

# 4. Follow-up cadence

| Channel | Touches | Spacing | What each adds | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn (cold → connected) | public comment → connect → DM → FU1 → FU2/park | comment 1–7 days before the request; DM within 1 day of accept; FU1 +4–6 working days; FU2 +7–10 working days | asset; new angle or voice note; open door | any "no", "kein Bedarf", objection; silence after FU2 → park for 60–90 days |
| LinkedIn (existing 1st-degree) | DM → FU1 → park | +5 / +10 working days | as above | same |
| Email, press (editorial route) | pitch → 1 FU | +3–5 working days, new element (data, case, date) | — | after FU1; the next pitch needs a new angle |
| Email, NGO/funder | only expected routes (contact form, call, intro); no cold sequence | — | — | see section 6 |
| Instagram | 2–3 comments → DM → 1 FU | FU +5–7 days | asset (sample letter or 45s screen recording) | after FU1; honor "email only" in the bio |

Channel switch: allowed once, to an *expected* route with a new reason (e.g., LinkedIn silence → the press desk with a news angle). Never after a "no". Re-engagement: only with a new trigger, once per quarter at most.

**Pipeline stages:** Identifiziert → Aufgewärmt (comment/like with substance) → Verbunden → Angeschrieben → Antwort → Gespräch → **Lead (dated commitment: pilot page, share date, intro sent, segment, application invite)** → Aktiv → Geparkt/Abgeschlossen. Count leads, not replies.

**Weekly target, solo with limited energy (inference):** 4–5 blocks of 30–40 minutes. Per week: 10 substantive public comments, 8–10 new first touches (start with 1st-degree contacts), all due follow-ups, 1 warm-intro ask, 1–2 calls. Review guardrail: build custom assets (campaign page, screen recording) only *after* interest, or for at most 3 top targets per week. Otherwise asset building eats the outreach time. One persona and one angle per week (the journalist contact's batch logic). If a 10–15-person batch produces zero substantive replies, change the angle, not the volume.

# 5. Call preparation

# 6. Legal, platform and ethics risks (risk guidance, not legal advice)

- **UWG §7 (email):** German courts read "Werbung" broadly. *Precision added in review after checking both case summaries:* in OLG Dresden (24.06.2024, 4 U 168/23), an event organizer emailed a beverage company asking for free drinks **in exchange for displaying its promotional material**. The court treated this as promoting the sender's own services, so the email needed prior consent. In OLG Frankfurt (06.10.2016, 6 U 54/16), a **car dealership** sent a customer SMS about a charitable vote, and the court counted it as advertising because it indirectly polished the dealer's image. Neither case involved a non-profit civic sender asking for an unpaid share, so the transfer to BnB is *inference*, not a ruling. The cautious reading still holds: a free, non-profit tool does not make a cold email to NGOs, creators or funders clearly safe, especially if it offers anything in return (visibility, logo, mention). Prefer LinkedIn conversations, warm intros, contact forms and official application routes. Press pitches with a genuine story to an editorial address are a different, lower-risk case, but they still need a story, not promotion. Never send a cold email sequence.
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies prohibit spam, including "inappropriately commercial" or "gratuitously repetitive" messages and promotional use of invitations. Restrictions follow ignored or spam-marked invitations, and withdrawn invites cannot be resent for up to three weeks. Meta's spam policy restricts repetitive and unwanted commercial messaging. Never automate or copy-paste identical texts, and keep each message individual.
- **Werbekennzeichnung:** if a creator receives anything of value (money, a product, a reciprocal promotion), the post may need an ad label (Medienanstalten guidance). An unpaid civic share is not automatically advertising; never offer hidden benefits.
- **Pressekodex:** never offer anything to journalists in exchange for coverage, never ask for favorable framing, and offer verifiable data.
- **DSGVO:** a private contact tracker with public professional data is plausible under legitimate interest. Keep it minimal and delete parked contacts after a set period. If you process data for direct marketing, an Art. 21 objection must stop that processing immediately. Assess the Art. 14 information duty.
- **Ethics:** never invent traction, quotes or relationships. Sample letters written "in their name" must be clearly labeled as an example and never published.
