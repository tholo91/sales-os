# 1. Findings that change drafting (ranked)

1. **The "this tool exists" story is spent nationally. Pitch second-day stories.** dpa ran "Brief nach Berlin - So einfach geht der Kontakt zur Politik" on 2026-06-24, syndicated by t-online, Freie Presse, Mindener Tageblatt, RPR1 and others. The BnB press page also lists SZ, Handelsblatt, WiWo, Stern, t-online, Welt and regional papers, plus the podcasts/radio Lage der Nation, Der KI-Podcast (ARD Sounds), Deutschlandfunk Kultur, detektor.fm and FluxFM Berlin (live presentation 14.09.2026). Lage der Nation episode 478 is documented in the BnB repo (archive/OUTREACH.md); the press page itself names no episode. National outlets have done the founder/launch angle. New pitches need something fresh: a data finding, an outcome (did politicians reply?), a local pattern, an election/budget hook, a campaign with an NGO, or a counter-thesis. Local outlets that did not run the dpa copy can still take a local founder or user story. *verified-source (dpa via t-online) + repo observation (brief-nach-berlin/press/, brief-nach-berlin.de/presse)*
2. **Pitch journalists by 1:1 email. Use LinkedIn and Bluesky to warm up and to watch them.** In DACH, 89% of journalists prefer to be contacted by email, about 21% by phone and only 8% via social media (news aktuell Medien-Trendmonitor 2025, n>1,000; re-checked 2026-10-04). Globally, 97% prefer email (Cision 2026). DACH journalists use Instagram (47%) and LinkedIn (46%) professionally, Bluesky 11%, X 27%, and mostly passively: monitoring, research, inspiration. For Thomas, who works LinkedIn first: comment on the journalist's piece or post on LinkedIn, then send the pitch to their published editorial email address. Send a LinkedIn DM pitch only when the person invites pitches there, or to creator journalists and newsletter makers who run their business on LinkedIn. *verified-source*
3. **Make relevance to the audience the first line.** 82% of journalists reject pitches that don't fit their audience or beat, and most say at most a quarter of pitches they get are relevant (Cision 2026). 88% immediately discard off-beat pitches, and 78% say pitches get traction when they directly affect their audience's community (Muck Rack 2026, US-heavy). Irrelevant PR mail is the top personal burden for DACH journalists (56%, news aktuell 2025). Rule: the first sentence names *their* audience or region and the second gives the fact. *verified-source*
4. **Local is the highest-leverage segment, and BnB's own data shows it.** Founder data (STATS.md, as of 2026-06-09): the Weser-Kurier article on 30/31 May produced 132 letters in 4 days, 42% of an 18-day volume. After the Lage der Nation mention, Thomas's own outreach drafts reported "über 150 Briefe erstellt, 1.600 Aufrufe in 3 Tagen" (BnB repo archive/OUTREACH.md). ~~"increased usage about six-fold"~~ *[REVIEW: removed, no source found in the BnB repo]*. *Founder-reported, not independently verified.* Implication: prioritise local/regional editors (city of an active user cluster, Bremen before the 2027 Bürgerschaft election) and podcasts with a loyal political audience over national desks that already covered BnB. *inference from founder data*
5. **Lead with facts, not storytelling, and have the kit ready.** DACH journalists want fact-based, transparent communication (53%), reliable data/studies (42%), exclusive content (37%), quick answers (36%) and expert access (36%). Storytelling ranks low (26%). 75% call background info and fact sheets essential, 67% value photos, and 76% want a direct download link rather than "on request" (news aktuell 2025). The BnB press page currently offers logos, screenshots and background "on request" (re-checked 2026-10-04). A downloadable kit (fact sheet, founder portrait and screenshots with rights notice, number definitions) removes friction. **Fix the press page before the next pitch:** it currently states as fact that handwritten letters get genuine attention in MdB offices "unlike emails or petitions". That is an unsourced impact claim of exactly the kind section 6 forbids; rephrase as Thomas's hypothesis or cite a source [PRUEFEN: Beleg oder umformulieren]. *verified-source + observed 2026-10-04*
6. **Define every number before any journalist sees it.** The homepage counter ("3.023 Briefe seit Mitte Mai 2026") counts *generated drafts*. Per STATS.md (2026-06-09): of 91 feedback responses, 53 said they mailed the letter (76% of the 70 who answered the question; only 8% of all drafts confirmed). Topic/PLZ "Themensignale" are stored only for users who opted in, with no letter text, and may not yet be live. Every number in a pitch needs its definition, date and sample, e.g. "3.023 erstellte Briefentwürfe (Stand [DATUM]); wie viele verschickt wurden, weiß ich nur aus freiwilligem Feedback". Missing or unverifiable sources lead 33% of journalists to drop a pitch (Muck Rack 2025). *repo-fact + verified-source*
7. **Keep it short, use the subject line as the headline, link rather than attach.** 69% prefer pitches under 200 words, 62% prefer email pitches and 78% prefer receiving pitches before noon (Muck Rack 2025, US-heavy; treat the timing figure as weak for Germany). Irrelevance (86%), promotional tone (71%), mass-email feel (50%), length (43%) and missing sources (33%) get pitches deleted. Write 120-180 words of body text with a link to the kit. *[REVIEW: "attach nothing" downgraded.]* In DACH 76% prefer a download link, but 62% also accept email attachments (news aktuell 2025). So the link is the default, and one small photo is acceptable; never send large files. *verified-source; transfer to DE is inference*
8. **Send one follow-up that adds something new, then stop.** Half of journalists find one follow-up fine, ideally 3-7 days later (Muck Rack 2025). Creator journalists tolerate a follow-up within the first week (82%, Muck Rack creator report 2025). Calling to ask "Ist meine Mail angekommen?" is a classic irritant in German practice (news aktuell/Faktenkontor 2017, dated; only 19% then named phone follow-up as annoying, but the "did you receive it" call is the canonical example). *[REVIEW: the 2017 figure was not re-checked; treat it as practitioner-consensus.]* The one-follow-up rule rests on US-heavy data. No German source measures reply-rate effects.

In DACH, 37% list exclusive content among their expectations. The Presserat dropped its embargo guideline in 2006, so honouring a Sperrfrist is voluntary and promotional reasons don't justify one. Offer a data point or user story to one outlet with a clear window ("bis Donnerstag exklusiv für Sie, danach biete ich es anderen an"). *verified-source (surveys); Wikipedia secondary for the 2006 change*

# 2. Sub-persona playbooks

Shared defaults: Sie in a first email to editorial staff unless their own channel signals du. du is fine for podcast hosts, newsletter creators and youth formats who use it publicly. Founder voice ("ich"). One angle and one ask. No donation link in a media pitch. Link to the press kit; no attachment.

**(a) National politics / Hauptstadt journalists** (Spiegel/Zeit/SZ/FAZ politics desks, Table.Media, Tagesspiegel Background, dpa Inland)
- *Flooded with:* party and NGO statements, government PR, think-tank studies, lobby invitations. Sitting weeks are peak load.
- *Value:* exclusive information, new data on the state of democracy, access to people, a counter-intuitive finding. A tool is not news to them.
- *Replies to:* a defensible data point or pattern tied to a parliamentary moment, e.g. "Vor der Haushaltswoche: Worüber Menschen ihren Abgeordneten schreiben" (only with opt-in topic data, n stated), or "Petition vs. persönlicher Brief" with a clean comparison frame.
- *Kills it:* "Startup stellt sich vor", repeating the dpa angle, impact claims ("Briefe verändern Politik"), anything that reads as SPD-near activism.
- *Channel/entry:* warm intro from someone they know (paths via a journalist contact and a connector contact) [PRUEFEN: welche Intros sind real zugesagt?] > personal author email after a substantive LinkedIn or Bluesky reaction to their piece > desk address. Pitch in a non-sitting week or early in the week. Avoid 24-27 Nov unless the pitch is for that week.
- *Structure:* subject = headline | 1 sentence why them (their recent piece) | 2-3 sentences finding + definition + date | 1 sentence who Thomas is | 1 question.
- *Smallest ask:* "Wäre das für Sie als Datenpunkt interessant? Dann schicke ich die Auswertung mit Methodik."
- *Lead =* they request the dataset/methodology, call him for a quote, or put him on a list of sources for a planned piece.
- *Solo-founder fit [REVIEW]:* lowest priority for Thomas right now. Pitch this segment only once a clean, defined data cut exists [PRUEFEN: gibt es eine belastbare Auswertung mit n und Methodik?]. Until then, local outlets, dpa regional desks (finding 13) and podcasts give more letters per hour.

**(b) Local / regional newsrooms** (Weser-Kurier, buten un binnen, NOZ, WAZ local desks, Lokalradio)
- *Flooded with:* municipal press releases, club news, event notices. Small teams and high time pressure.
- *Value:* people from their area, regional numbers, service value for readers, a face for a photo.
- *Replies to:* "[Stadt]: [n] Menschen haben seit Mai einen Brief an Politik entworfen; worüber sie schreiben" (needs a PLZ aggregate, normalised per population, with minimum cell size), a local user who agreed to talk, the local MdB/MdL as addressee, or Bremen Bürgerschaftswahl 30 May 2027.
- *Kills it:* national copy with only the city name swapped, numbers too small to publish, no protagonist.
- *Channel/entry:* the local desk email or the reporter who covers civic topics. Local reporters often answer the phone, but email first. A local user or NGO who introduces Thomas is the strongest path.
- *Smallest ask:* "Hätten Sie Interesse an der Zahl für [Stadt] und an einem Kontakt zu einer Briefschreiberin aus [Stadt], die darüber sprechen möchte?"
- *Lead =* an article or radio piece, plus a reusable contact for the next local hook (election, budget). The Weser-Kurier effect suggests this is the best "letters per hour of outreach" segment [PRUEFEN: aktuelle Daten].

**(c) Podcast hosts and podcast editorial teams** (politics/society: news shows with listener sections, interview formats, regional politics podcasts)
- *Flooded with:* authors' and companies' guest pitches built on generic templates.
- *Value:* a guest who gives listeners a takeaway, a story with tension, a clear voice and good audio. Hosts also value guests who share the episode afterwards.
- *Replies to:* a concrete episode idea with a title and 3 talking points tied to the show's past episodes. For BnB: "Wie schreibe ich meiner Abgeordneten so, dass es gelesen wird?" (service), "Was 3.000 Briefentwürfe über politischen Frust verraten" (data, only with clean data), "Warum ein KI-Tool auf Handschrift setzt" (counter-thesis).
- *Kills it:* "Ich würde gern mal in Ihren Podcast kommen", no sign he has listened, talking about the product instead of the listener.
- *Channel/entry:* the show's guest/contact email or the producer, not the host's personal DM. Shows that already mentioned BnB (Lage der Nation, Der KI-Podcast/ARD, Deutschlandfunk Kultur per press page) get a short update note in their listener channel. That is not a guest pitch.
- *Smallest ask:* "Passt das Thema grundsätzlich zu euch? Dann schicke ich drei mögliche Fragen."
- *Lead =* a booking, or the producer saving him in their guest database for a related episode.

**(d) Newsletter makers** (Substack/Steady/LinkedIn newsletters on democracy, politics, civic tech, Bremen/regional)
- *Flooded with:* tool submissions and "please feature us" requests.
- *Value:* things their readers can *do*, original insight, a good link with a sentence of context, and personal relationships. A Muck Rack report finds 1 in 3 journalists now publish independently.
- *Replies to:* a link plus one fact they can paste, or a guest-contribution offer on a specific question. Personal tone; LinkedIn DM is acceptable when they publish on LinkedIn.
- *Kills it:* asking for a feature without reading the newsletter; long pitches; assuming they will promote for free.
- *Smallest ask:* "Wäre das was für deine Mitmach-Ecke? Dann schick ich dir zwei Sätze zum Übernehmen." *[REVIEW: "Kein Muss" removed; workspace/voice.md rejects automatic no-pressure endings.]*
- *Lead =* a mention with link, a guest piece, or a recurring "Mitmach-Tipp" slot. Ask what they need; if they offer paid placement, that is advertising and must be labelled (see section 6).

**(e) Radio / public broadcasters incl. youth formats** (NDR/Radio Bremen/WDR regional radio, Deutschlandfunk/DLF Nova, funk, ARD youth waves, tagesschau/ZDFheute social)
- *Flooded with:* institutional PR and NGO campaigns. They are bound by balance and independence from state and parties.
- *Value:* service value ("So erreichen Sie Ihre Abgeordnete"), a voice for O-Ton, a young protagonist, a debate ("Bringen Briefe an Politiker:innen etwas?"), timing near elections.
- *Replies to:* an angle about a listener's problem rather than about the tool; a person available for a phone interview at the morning show time; a young user for youth formats [PRUEFEN: gibt es junge Nutzer:innen mit Einwilligung?].
- *Kills it:* anything that sounds like an election-campaign tool or party-near. Public broadcasters avoid promoting a single product, so BnB appears as one example within a theme.
- *Channel/entry:* the editorial email of the specific programme (morning show, Landespolitik, Netz/Digital), regional first. Thomas's Bremen base fits Radio Bremen and NDR Niedersachsen before the 2027 elections.
- *Smallest ask:* "Ich stehe für ein kurzes Telefon-O-Ton-Gespräch zur Verfügung."
- *Lead =* a phone interview or on-air mention, then a later return for election coverage.

**(f) Civic-tech / digital journalists** (netzpolitik.org, heise/c't, t3n, Golem, digital desks of national outlets, Tagesspiegel Background Digitalisierung)
- *Flooded with:* AI product launches, "democracy tech" hype, startup PR.
- *Value:* technical honesty, data protection, open source, risks and failure modes, critical stance. netzpolitik.org accepts substantive tips via its contact address and authors' pages.
- *Replies to:* design decisions with a civic rationale: no letter-text storage, EU-hosted model (dpa says only "KI aus Frankreich"; Mistral is named in BnB's own DSGVO docs, not by dpa) [PRUEFEN: aktueller Stand], human-in-the-loop handwriting as protection against AI letter floods, open adaptation guide (ADAPT_TO_YOUR_COUNTRY.md). Also open failures: "Was beim KI-Briefentwurf schiefging."
- *Kills it:* marketing language, "KI revolutioniert Demokratie", unverifiable privacy claims.
- *Smallest ask:* "Wenn Sie über KI und politische Kommunikation recherchieren: Ich lege gern offen, wie das System gebaut ist, inklusive Schwächen."
- *Lead =* a background talk, a critical piece that names BnB as a careful example, or a code/methodology request.

# 4. Follow-up cadence

- **Journalists (a, b, e, f):** at most 1 follow-up after 4-6 working days, by email in the same thread. It must add one new element: a fresh number, a protagonist now available, a date hook, or an exclusive window ("Ich biete es bis Freitag nur Ihnen an"). Then stop. Pitch again only with a *new angle*, at the earliest 4-6 weeks later or at a verified hook. Never chase by phone or LinkedIn DM unless there is an existing relationship. A reply such as "passt gerade nicht" ends the thread, and Thomas should ask once whether a future hook (e.g. the 2027 elections) would be welcome.
- **Podcasts:** 1 follow-up after 7-10 days, adding one concrete question listeners could ask or a recent episode hook. Booking lead times are often weeks *(inference)*, so pitch election-related ideas 6-10 weeks ahead.
- **Newsletter makers:** 1 follow-up after a week, or none if the first message was only a share offer and got no reply. Creator journalists expect follow-up within the first week (Muck Rack 2025).
- **After coverage (the real lead):** thank them the same day, share the piece publicly with a tag, and send the impact note later if real ("Nach Ihrem Beitrag kamen [ZAHL] Entwürfe aus [STADT]" [PRUEFEN]). Then add them to a small "relevant updates" list that Thomas writes by hand, at most once a quarter and only with real news.
- **Channel switch:** email for pitches; LinkedIn/Bluesky for public engagement with their work. Phone only for radio interviews, for an existing relationship, or when the journalist offered it.

# 5. Call preparation

**What they will ask (prepare 1-2 sentence answers):**
1. What exactly does the number count? (Drafts vs mailed letters, since when, source, date.)
2. Who are you, who finances this, are you partisan? (Solo project, WE AID gGmbH support [PRUEFEN: exakte Form], donations; former SPD MdB internships named openly; routing by responsibility, not party.)

# 6. Legal, platform and ethics risks (risk guidance, not legal advice)

- **UWG §7 (email):** promotional email without prior consent is unlawful even B2B. A purely editorial pitch from a non-commercial civic project is not clearly "Werbung", and a court has held that genuine press research is not advertising. But the classification is unsettled for pitches, and a donation appeal, campaign-product promotion or a mass send pushes the message towards advertising. Keep media pitches purely editorial and individually sent to published editorial or author addresses, and honour any "bitte keine Mails" at once.
- **DSGVO:** journalist contact notes in workspace/ require minimal data, a legitimate-interest basis, Art. 14 information if the data came from public sources, and Art. 21 objection honoured. **Letter topics are political opinions, a special category under Art. 9.** Publish only aggregates from opted-in Themensignale with a minimum cell size [PRUEFEN: Schwelle festlegen, z. B. n≥10]. Never share an individual case without that person's explicit consent.
- **Pressekodex:** Ziffer 7 separates editorial content from advertising; Ziffer 15 concerns benefits. Offer no gifts or "Gegenleistung", don't ask to approve the article (quotes only), and don't ask for a donation link in coverage.
- **Werbekennzeichnung:** if a newsletter or podcast offers *paid* placement, or Thomas ever pays, it is advertising and must be labelled (Medienanstalten guidance). Never blend it with an editorial pitch.
