# Delta: cold first-touch DM and email evidence

Scope: only what is new, more current, better sourced or contradicting versus `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, `research/2026-10-28-linkedin-outreach-check.md`, `research/2026-10-02-follow-up-evidence.md`, `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`, `knowledge/channels/email.md`, `knowledge/channels/linkedin.md` and the claims register. Public pages only; no contact, no profiles. Pages were read with a fetch tool that summarises text; figures marked "read via summary" were not checked in a browser. The honest headline: no randomized study of cold first-touch wording (opener, ask type, subject) was found. The causal evidence that exists is adjacent (survey invitations, request channel, AI-use perception).

## Contradicts or weakens existing guidance

- `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, finding 9 and "Review 2026-10-04" change 1: it says the "free/demo lower opens by up to 17.9%" figure was not on the cited Gong page and removes it. The page currently says "Buzzwords & numbers reduce open rates by up to 17.9%" (claim 1). The removal was right for the "free/demo" wording and wrong for the figure itself. The figure is an "up to" opens number from an undescribed dataset; reinstating it as direction only, not as a rule, is the correct reading.
- `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`, `## Drafting card` Length and `## Evidence boundary` ("shorter first messages correlate with more replies"): the correlation is vendor-specific and weak in Hunter's 11M-email data (r = -0.09) and non-monotonic in the 2026 report (claim 5); the only randomized test found no length effect (claim 6). The length numbers are style choices for the medium, not evidence-backed optima.
- `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, finding 4 (Instantly: "fewer than four touches gives up too early") against Hunter 2026 (three or more follow-ups lower the average, claim 2) and the Instantly sequence page itself, which gives both "at least 4" and "3 total, then pause". Vendors disagree; the repo's 3-touch rule is supported by the newer Hunter cut but not proven.
- `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, finding 11 (small batches 5.8% vs 2.1%, attributed to Woodpecker): the same pair of numbers appears on Hunter's 2025 report page, so the origin is unclear (claim origin unverified).
- `research/2026-10-28-linkedin-outreach-check.md`, row "AI-sounding copy: no independent study": weakened. Peer-reviewed work now exists on perceived AI use (claims 9 and 10), but it covers known or disclosed AI use at work, not recipient detection of AI text in a cold DM.
- `research/2026-10-28-linkedin-outreach-check.md`, row "Interest CTA vs meeting ask": still rests on the Gong 304k page; the searches only surfaced a vendor blog re-telling it, and no original or independent test was found. It stays "email only, vendor, observational".

## Not found or unverified

- No randomized or preregistered study of cold first-touch wording (opener, ask type, interest question vs meeting ask, subject line) in a comparable setting was found. Searches for academic field experiments surfaced only audit studies of discrimination (for example Milkman, Akinola and Chugh on emails to professors, not opened) and survey-invitation tests.
- "Economics of cold emailing" (ResearchGate, 2025 preprint): the page returned 403; a search summary mentions 5,187 emails and a 15% average response rate; design, authors and peer review unverified.
- Whether a plain one-to-two-sentence "what this is" line helps busy recipients judge quickly: no direct test found. Indirect only: Gong's "stop pitching" (claim 1) and Hunter's survey that "too pushy or sales-focused" is the top complaint (claim 11); both are vendor data and neither isolates a neutral descriptor.
- Interest question versus meeting ask: the Gong 304k CTA page was not re-opened this session; a blog retelling it was seen; independent confirmation not found.
- Flynn and Lake (2008) and Bohns (2016) on people underestimating compliance with direct help requests: seen only as search-snippet summaries; originals not opened; not used as a claim.
- Current LinkedIn behaviour as of November 2026: no official announcement about messaging, AI-detection or new invite caps was found. Vendor posts claim tighter enforcement since late 2025 and weekly caps of about 100; the official help pages state no weekly number (claim 12). Sales Navigator "Message Assist" limits (reported as 50 AI-assisted messages per month) appeared only in a community post snippet and are unverified.
- Gmail and Yahoo sender rules: not re-checked here; existing `knowledge/channels/email.md` citations stand.
- German-language cold DM or email experiments, and effects specific to German recipients (du/Sie, formality): none found.
- Instantly 2026 benchmark report (reply rates, 58/42 step split): only read via the vendor's blog restatement; no sample size or period stated there.
- Lavender 2026 benchmark page (231,818 emails, cutoff 2026-02-04, updated 2026-03-30) states no findings on length, subject or personalization in the readable text; the findings in the 2026-10-04 note come from other Lavender pages.
