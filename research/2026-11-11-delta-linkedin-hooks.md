# LinkedIn hooks and post structure: delta to the 2026-10 guidance

Lane 3 of 6. Reads as delta to `research/2026-10-04-linkedin-posts-2026-delta.md`, `research/2026-10-01-linkedin-writing.md`, `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`, `knowledge/foundations/short-punchy-content.md` and the claims register. Already established there and not repeated: LLM-embedding feed (2026-03-12), AI-slop and engagement-bait statements, no official link penalty, 140/210 character tool estimates, keyword-comment CTAs, post cadence, saves and sends.

Scope of the evidence: LinkedIn publishes no rule for the "see more" cut, for hook wording, for post length or for links. The only controlled evidence on first lines comes from news-headline experiments (Upworthy, Washington Post), which measure clicks on a headline, not reading of a LinkedIn post, and are US English. Transfer to a German personal profile is an assumption in every such case.

## Contradicts or weakens existing guidance

- `research/2026-10-04-linkedin-posts-2026-delta.md`, finding 1 (write so an LLM can tell the topic): the 2026-03-12 blog lists post text only as a retrieval input. The ranker paper (#1) does not say Feed SR reads post text. The topic-clarity rule stays a sensible inference, but it is weaker than "LinkedIn ranks with LLM embeddings of your post text". Label it inference.
- `research/2026-10-04-linkedin-posts-2026-delta.md`, finding 7, and `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`, `## First lines` (line 1 about 70, lines 1+2 about 140 characters): already labelled practitioner. New: vendor tools disagree, some describe the cut by lines, not characters (#3). Keep as a working rule; do not describe 140 as an established number.
- `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`, `## Edit the opening and ending together` ("A clear answer can be stronger than withheld information. Reject vague suspense") and `## First lines` ("Prefer one concrete scene"): the concreteness curve (#5) supports "not vague", but also shows that a headline revealing everything can lose clicks. Add the other half: leave one concrete thing for the body.
- `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`, `## Links` ("accepting a possible reach cost") and `## Evidence and limits`: a newer vendor read (#8) says personal profiles show almost no penalty. It is one undisclosed-sample source, so the rule stands, but the cost may be near zero for Thomas.
- `research/2026-10-04-linkedin-posts-2026-delta.md`, finding 2 and the "Avoid" list under hook patterns: the 40% figure has no baseline or scope, and LinkedIn's criterion is substance (#9). The list of AI-template phrases is the repo's inference and does not map to any published classifier rule; say so where it appears.
- `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`, `## Timing as a test`: "Being available for genuine replies is a practical choice, not proof of a mandatory first-hour boost" is consistent with #1-#2; no change.

Draft hook checklist (6 lines; items 1-4 rest on primary study or platform data, items 5-6 are working rules):

1. The first lines say something specific about the topic: not vague, and not the whole answer. (Headline experiments, 8,977 tests; US news; transfer assumed.)
2. Use common, plain words and short phrases; do not trust your own taste for "polished". (30,000+ field experiments; English.)
3. The post contains a point of view or detail only you can give; LinkedIn's slop criterion is missing perspective or substance, not style. (LinkedIn 2026-09-15.)
4. Name a real problem plainly if there is one; never add negative words for effect (small effect, news clicks only, civic neutrality).
5. Working rule: the promise is complete in about the first 2 lines (about 140 characters); there is no official number, check the phone preview.
6. Working rule: no "question" or "number" tricks as rules; test one change per post and log it.
