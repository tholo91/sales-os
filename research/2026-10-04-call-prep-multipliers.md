# 1. Findings that change drafting (ranked)

1. **A call is only progress if it ends in a commitment that costs the other person something: time (a dated next call/test), reputation (a named intro, a share, a quote) or money (grant step, donation).** Compliments, "klingt spannend", "schick mal Infos" are not leads. If Thomas cannot say what happens next and when, the call did not advance. *practitioner-consensus* (Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test, summarised by [mtlynch.io](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)); fits the repo's existing pilot/next-step rule. Change: `prepare-call` should define the *target commitment per call type* before any questions.
2. **Discuss next steps explicitly, but earn them with value talk, not a feature tour.** Gong (8,382 deals, 2021): close rate ~20% when next steps were discussed vs ~5% without; win rates fell sharply as feature-talk time grew. *verified-source, vendor-observational, older, B2B* ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/driving-next-steps-isnt-enough-this-is-what-really-moves-deals-forward); figures re-checked 2026-10-04; correlational, so "next steps discussed" may simply mark deals that were already going well). Transfer to unpaid multiplier calls is *inference*. Change: BnB pitch = one story + one demo moment, then their goals; no walkthrough of every flow (campaigns, Europe, reviews).
3. **Fewer, better questions; short monologues; lots of back-and-forth.** Gong 2025 (326k calls, updated 2026-03-04): won calls ~57% seller talk vs 62% lost; ~15-16 questions in won vs ~20 in lost; top performers create more back-and-forth. *verified-source, vendor-observational, B2B paid-deal calls* ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio); figures re-checked 2026-10-04). [Review: downgraded] The 57/62% split is a B2B sales benchmark; do not treat any percentage as a target for a 20-minute civic call. What transfers (inference): ask first, keep monologues short, let them talk about their work. Change: max 5 prepared questions per call, no monologue over ~2 minutes (the 2-min pitch is the ceiling), pitch only after their first answers.
4. **Open with an up-front contract: purpose, their agenda, your agenda, time, and what happens at the end (including that "passt nicht" is fine).** *practitioner-consensus* ([Sandler](https://go.sandler.com/eam/insights/blog/categories/professional-development/up-front-contracts-the-simple-habit-behind-more-/)). Change: every call brief gets a scripted first 60 seconds that names the hoped-for outcome aloud, so the close at minute 25 is not a surprise.
5. **Ask for intros by name with a forwardable blurb (double opt-in), not "kennst du jemanden?".** The connector forwards a 5-10 sentence self-contained text; the target says yes before the intro. *practitioner-consensus* ([Fred Wilson/AVC](https://avc.com/2009/11/the-double-optin-introduction/), older but canonical; [Auren Hoffman](https://auren.substack.com/p/how-to-ask-for-an-introduction-the)). Repo evidence: in an advisory call with a connector contact (debrief 2026-10-01), a concrete ask for contacts produced named people and a warm network intro (details in private workspace) — one interaction, but it is the strongest result recorded.
6. **For journalists, the call is a source relationship, not a pitch:** they want relevance to their beat, a credible source and verifiable facts; they decide the story. Muck Rack 2025 (via secondary reports): irrelevance (86%), overly promotional tone (71%) and missing/unverifiable sources (33%) are the top reasons pitches get rejected; journalists want pitches anchored by verified sources, third-party data and credible experts (figures re-checked in PR News 2026-10-04; global/US-heavy sample, not German-specific). *verified-source via secondary* ([PR News 2025-06-18](https://www.prnewsonline.com/the-state-of-journalism-in-2025-insights-to-optimize-your-media-relations/); [Buchanan PR 2025-07-09](https://buchananpr.com/takeaways-from-muck-racks-2025-state-of-journalism-report/); 2026 edition gated: [Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/research/state-of-journalism)). Change: prepare **one Kernbotschaft in one sentence**, three proof points with source, a "what I cannot say" list, and ask about format/Autorisierung ([wissenschaftskommunikation.de](https://www.wissenschaftskommunikation.de/7-tipps-zur-vorbereitung-auf-ein-interview-239/), 2018, still standard German practice).
7. **Foundations reject most enquiries for fit, not quality; the Vorgespräch is for checking fit, not pitching.** Hamburg foundations guide (PDF metadata: created 2008, revised 2019): "nach Schätzungen bis zu 90%" of enquiries are sorted out because they lie outside the foundation's focus, are poorly prepared or unconvincing. [Review: downgraded] This is an *estimate* in a ~2008 regional guide, not measured data; use it as a direction, never quote it as a statistic. The same guide says most questions should be answered by own research first, and by phone only "kurz und gezielt einzelne Fragen", not a whole question catalogue. *verified-source (sector guide, old, estimate)* ([Bundesverband Deutscher Stiftungen PDF](https://www.stiftungen.org/fileadmin/stiftungen_org/Verband/Was_wir_tun/Veranstaltungen/AK-Foerderstiftungen/Leitfaden-fuer-Foerderanfragen-an-Stiftungen.pdf)); some foundations require a phone Vorgespräch or a Projektskizze first ([Bürgergesellschaft](https://www.buergergesellschaft.de/praxishilfen/erfolgreich-foerdermittel-einwerben/besonderheiten-von-foerdernden-einrichtungen/stiftungen)). Change: funder call target = "Is BnB förderfähig here, through which Träger, and may I send a 2-page Skizze by [date]?"
8. **Creators protect creative control and audience trust above everything.** CreatorIQ (2025-07-09): 99% of creators care about creative control; product quality is the No. 1 reason to work with a brand (84%). *vendor survey, paid brand deals, re-checked 2026-10-04* [Review: "relationships beat one-off deals" not re-verified; the survey is about paid partnerships, so BnB (no budget, no product to send) can only borrow the creative-control point] ([CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/blog/what-creators-want-from-brand-partnerships-creatoriq-connect)). Change: in creator calls, ask what *their* audience does after a political video and offer a format they control; never bring a script.
9. **Calibrated "Wie/Was" questions turn objections and vague interest into concrete conditions; avoid "Warum" (sounds accusatory).** *practitioner-consensus* ([Black Swan Group, 2024-12-02](https://www.blackswanltd.com/newsletter/taking-the-mystery-out-of-calibrated-questions)). Already in repo for discovery; extend to closing: "Was müsste passieren, damit ...?", "Wie würdest du das bei euch intern vorschlagen?"
10. **Never record or AI-transcribe a call without prior, informed agreement of everyone.** §201 StGB criminalises recording the non-public spoken word without authorisation; a 2026-08-21 Dr. Datenschutz article discusses AI meeting transcription under both §201 StGB and DSGVO and says consent is only valid with comprehensive prior information [Review: softened; whether pure live transcription without stored audio is a "Tonträger" recording is legally debated, so treat it as if it were] ([gesetze-im-internet.de](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__201.html); [Dr. Datenschutz](https://www.dr-datenschutz.de/ki-transkription-im-meeting/)). *verified-source (statute) + legal practitioner commentary.* Repo relevance: both stored BnB debriefs come from transcripts; the call brief needs a consent line.

# 2. Sub-persona playbooks (call types)

Common to all: Thomas speaks as "ich" (solo project, supported by WE AID gGmbH [PRUEFEN: exact wording of the support relationship]). Proof he may cite only with label: public counter "3.023 Briefe geschrieben" on 2026-10-04 [PRUEFEN: counts drafts or mailed letters?]; ~700 letters in one month and a Lage der Nation mention are *historical, founder/README-reported*.

## 2a. Journalist / podcast Vorgespräch
- **Flooded with:** off-topic, promotional, mass-sent pitches; startup "world first" claims; AI-tool announcements.
- **Values:** a story for *their* audience, a credible accessible source, verifiable numbers, a news hook (Anlass), people affected (a letter writer, a reply from an office).
- **Engages when:** Thomas gives a tight angle and real material (data excerpt, a willing letter writer, a documented reply) and accepts their framing.
- **Kills it:** asking for coverage or a link; unverifiable counts; partisan framing; demanding to approve the article (only quotes may be authorised).
- **Entry:** warm intro (e.g. a media contact offered by a connector contact) > reply to their own piece > 1:1 email with headline subject (advice from a journalist contact in the workspace) > LinkedIn.
- **Questions:** "Für welches Format/welche Rubrik denkst du gerade?" · "Was bräuchtest du, damit das für deine Redaktion trägt — Zahlen, eine Briefschreiberin, eine Reaktion aus einem Abgeordnetenbüro?" · "Gibt es einen Anlass, an dem du das festmachen würdest?" · "Wen sollte ich außer mir noch als Stimme anbieten?" · "Wie läuft bei euch die Autorisierung von Zitaten?"
- **Smallest fitting ask:** "Soll ich dir bis [Tag] [konkretes Material] schicken?"
- **Lead =** a dated material delivery they asked for, an interview slot, or a named colleague/Rubrik to route to.

## 2b. Creator / influencer collab call
- **Flooded with:** paid brand deals with rigid briefs, affiliate spam, NGO "please share" asks with no format.
- **Values:** creative control, audience trust, a format that performs, low effort, no partisan risk to their brand, (for some) payment.
- **Engages when:** the idea fits a thing they already make (e.g. a political explainer ending in "Schreib selbst einen Brief") and their community can act within minutes.
- **Kills it:** a script, a request to "promote", unclear disclosure, implying they endorse a party line.
- **Entry:** genuine comment history on their content (warm-contact-comment skill) > mutual connector (e.g. a connector contact who offered an intro to a creator) > DM.
- **Questions:** "Was machen deine Leute normalerweise, nachdem du ein politisches Thema erklärt hast?" · "Was hat bei dir schon mal echte Aktion ausgelöst — und was war Rohrkrepierer?" · "Wie würdest du das erzählen, wenn du es selbst ausprobierst?" · "Was wäre für dich ein No-Go?" · "Wie soll ich das kennzeichnen/formulieren, damit es für dich sauber ist?"
- **Smallest fitting ask:** "Magst du es einmal mit einem eigenen Anliegen ausprobieren, und wir sprechen danach 10 Minuten, ob es in ein Video passt?" or a prefilled campaign page for one of their topics.
- **Lead =** creator tested it themselves, or one dated piece (story/video) on their terms, with disclosure agreed.

## 2c. NGO / Verein / Initiative partnership discovery
- **Flooded with:** tool vendors, petition platforms, "cooperation" requests that create work for them.
- **Values:** their campaign goals, supporter activation beyond signatures, staff time, data protection, reputation (no mass-mail backlash).
- **Engages when:** BnB fits a *specific upcoming campaign moment* and costs them almost nothing to set up.
- **Kills it:** generic "Partnerschaft"; anything that sounds like automated letter flooding; unclear data flows.
- **Entry:** person responsible for campaigns/mobilisation, ideally via a mutual contact or their public campaign.
- **Questions (Mom Test):** "Wann habt ihr zuletzt eure Unterstützer:innen um eine persönliche Aktion gebeten — was genau, und was kam dabei raus?" · "Woran hakt es bei solchen Aktionen meistens?" · "Welche Kampagne steht in den nächsten drei Monaten an?" · "Wer entscheidet bei euch, ob ein Tool in eine Kampagne kommt?" · "Was müsste für euch sicher sein (Datenschutz, Tonalität, Adressat)?"
- **Smallest fitting ask:** "Ich baue dir bis [Datum] eine vorbefüllte Kampagnenseite für [Thema] als Entwurf; du schaust sie dir an und sagst ja/nein."
- **Lead =** a named campaign + owner + date for a test page, or a meeting with the decider.

## 2d. Funder / Stiftung Vorgespräch
- **Flooded with:** misfit applications, vague ideas, organisations without a Träger.
- **Values:** fit with Förderzweck and current programme, a capable person/Träger, measurable outputs, sustainability after funding, co-funding.
- **Engages when:** Thomas has read guidelines and past grantees and asks fit questions rather than pitching.
- **Kills it:** asking "fördert ihr sowas?" without having read the site; impact claims ("mehr Demokratie") without measurement; unclear legal entity.
- **Entry:** named programme officer (public team page) > a warm network intro (details in private workspace) > general contact.
- **Before the call (research, not questions):** Förderzweck, current programme, past grantees, who may apply (some foundations fund individuals, many only gemeinnützige Körperschaften), deadlines, co-funding. Decide beforehand whether the applicant would be Thomas or WE AID gGmbH [PRUEFEN with WE AID].
- **Questions (max 3 by phone; the Hamburg guide warns against working through a catalogue):** "Passt ein Projekt wie meins eher in [Programm A] oder gar nicht?" · "Was wollt ihr am Ende einer Förderung messen können?" · "Ist eine Projektskizze der richtige nächste Schritt, und bis wann?" Only if not answerable from the website: "Geht das für euch über einen Träger wie WE AID gGmbH? [PRUEFEN]"
- **Smallest fitting ask:** permission to send a 2-page Skizze by a date, or the name of a better-fitting programme/funder.
- **Lead =** invited Skizze/application with deadline, or a named referral.

## 2e. Advisor / connector call (goal: specific intros)
- **Flooded with:** "pick your brain" requests with no follow-through.
- **Values:** being useful efficiently, seeing their advice used, reciprocity, not burning their own contacts.
- **Engages when:** Thomas brings a short list of 3-5 named targets or types, asks for honest critique, and reports back.
- **Kills it:** open-ended "wen kennst du so?"; never reporting results (a connector contact explicitly asked to hear what worked).
- **Questions:** "Wenn du an meiner Stelle wärst: welche drei Menschen würdest du diese Woche anschreiben?" · "Bei [Name]: darf ich dich erwähnen, oder magst du lieber selbst kurz fragen?" · "Was an meinem Pitch würde bei [Name] nicht ziehen?" · "Wo hast du Projekte wie meins scheitern sehen?" · "Womit kann ich dir konkret helfen?"
- **Smallest fitting ask:** "Darf ich dir bis morgen einen 5-Zeilen-Text schicken, den du an [Name] weiterleiten kannst?"
- **Lead =** a named intro with permission to reference them, or a forwardable blurb they agreed to send.

# 4. Follow-up cadence after a call

- **T+0-24 h:** recap (template D): their words, both commitments with dates, one correction invite. Attach only what was promised. This is the single most important follow-up.
- **By the promised date:** deliver the thing (Skizze, campaign page, blurb, data). Delivering on time is the follow-up that builds trust; never send it late with an apology chain.
- **Commitment date +3-5 working days, if silent:** one nudge that *adds* something: a finished draft they can just forward, a new data point [PRUEFEN], a short screen recording, or a two-option question ("Lieber [A] oder erst nach [Termin]?"). Journalists: one follow-up after 3-7 days is the accepted norm (Muck Rack 2025 via PR News).
- **+2-3 weeks:** last touch with a clean exit ("Ich lass das dann erst mal ruhen, melde mich gern wieder, wenn [konkreter Anlass]"). Then stop. No third nudge without new value or a real Anlass.
- **Connectors:** report back within ~2 weeks of each intro outcome (worked/didn't), regardless of result; this is what earns the next intro.
- **Channel switch:** stay in the channel where the call was agreed; switch only to the channel they used in the call (e.g. "schick's mir per Mail"). For an intro that stalls, ask the connector for the offered active intro instead of a parallel DM sequence (as in the 2026-10-01 connector debrief).

# 5. Call preparation

## 5.1 Ten-minute pre-call checklist
1. (2 min) Read the interaction file and last message; what did *they* say they want?
2. (2 min) Their last 2-3 public items (article, post, campaign, funding programme); pick one [SIGNAL] to reference in minute 1.
3. (1 min) Set the **target commitment** and an acceptable no-fit outcome.
4. (1 min) Pick 5 questions from the persona bank; star the 2 that must be asked.
5. (1 min) Pick the one demo moment and test it live (link works, PLZ result loads). Have a screenshot fallback.
6. (1 min) Check proof numbers on the live site today; mark anything unchecked [PRUEFEN].
7. (1 min) Prepare the intro ask: 1-3 *named* people and the blurb (template C).
8. (1 min) Decide recording: none, or ask beforehand in writing.

## 5.2 One-page call brief (template)
```
Person / Rolle / Organisation:      | Call-Typ: Journalist | Creator | NGO | Förderer | Connector
Wie zustande gekommen (Kanal, Satz der Antwort):
Ihr Anliegen in ihren Worten:       | [SIGNAL] für die Eröffnung:
Zielcommitment (Zeit/Reputation/Geld) + Datum:     | Akzeptables Nein:
5 Fragen (2 markiert):
Demo-Moment (+ was ich NICHT zeige):
Belege, die ich nennen darf (mit Quelle/Stand):     | [PRUEFEN]-Liste / No-Go-Aussagen:
Wahrscheinlichster Einwand + meine Wie/Was-Frage:

```

## 5.3 The first 60 seconds (up-front contract)
"Danke dir für die Zeit. Wir haben 20 Minuten, passt das noch? Mein Vorschlag: Erst würd ich gern verstehen, woran du bei [SIGNAL] gerade arbeitest, dann zeig ich dir eine Sache an Brief nach Berlin, und am Ende schauen wir, ob es einen nächsten Schritt gibt. Kann auch sein, dass es nicht passt, das sagst du mir dann einfach. Was muss für dich unbedingt rein?" [Review: aligned to 20 min (template A said 20, this said 25); the "a no is fine" element stays because it is the Sandler mechanism in a live call, but phrased as a request, not a reassurance formula] Then ask the first question, not the pitch.

## 5.4 Pitch: 30 seconds and 2 minutes
- **30 s:** Who + what it does in their words + proof with label + why them. "Ich bin Thomas aus Bremen. Brief nach Berlin hilft Leuten, aus politischem Frust einen persönlichen Brief zu machen: Anliegen beschreiben, PLZ eingeben, man bekommt die zuständige Stelle und einen Entwurf, den man selbst überarbeitet und abschickt. Kostenlos, überparteilich, ohne Account. [PRUEFEN: Zahl]. Ich spreche mit dir, weil [SIGNAL]."
- **2 min (only if asked, or after their first answers):** (1) Origin: the mother story — in instagram-start.md (draft, 2026-09-05) told in first person as Thomas's own mother [PRUEFEN: Thomas bestätigt, dass die Geschichte wörtlich so stimmt, bevor er sie live erzählt]: she was politically frustrated, did not know whom to write or how to turn frustration into a reasonable letter. (2) The barrier generalised: not apathy, but "wem und wie". (3) What it does + why the person stays in control (edit, handwrite, send). (4) Proof with labels + what is still unknown (no impact proof). (5) Founder credibility: political science background and Bundestag internship [PRUEFEN: Formulierung]. (6) The bridge: "Und deshalb frage ich mich, ob das für [ihr Ziel] passt."
- Rule: tailor step (6) per persona; drop (5) for creators, lead with (4) for journalists and funders.

## 5.5 The one demo moment
Live, on their topic: for NGOs, creators and connectors ask "Welches Thema nervt dich gerade?" and their PLZ (or a neutral sample PLZ). For journalists and funders do not ask for their personal political concern (professional neutrality; political opinions are Art. 9 data): use a prepared neutral example from their beat or region, e.g. a local issue like a Radweg or Schulsanierung. Show the recipient suggestion and the first lines of the draft. [Review: split by persona] Stop there; ask "Was würdest du jetzt erwarten?" For NGOs show a prefilled campaign page instead. Do not show: admin, Europe path, roadmap. Test both flows 10 minutes before; have screenshots if the live call fails. Note the draft arrives by email [PRUEFEN: how long it takes live, so the demo does not stall].

## 5.7 Asking for intros
Ask in the call, by name: "Du hast [Name] erwähnt. Wäre es okay, wenn ich dir heute einen kurzen Text schicke, den du weiterleiten kannst? Oder darf ich mich direkt mit Bezug auf dich melden?" Agree which variant (direct with name-drop vs active intro), then send template C the same day. One named person per ask; max three per call.

## 5.8 Close for one next step with a date
Summarise in their words, then: "Was wäre aus deiner Sicht ein sinnvoller nächster Schritt?" If vague, propose one concrete option with a date: "Ich schick dir bis Donnerstag X, du sagst mir bis [Tag] ja/nein — passt das?" Once they agree, ask "Wann und wo machst du das konkret?" and use their answer verbatim in the recap (see 6a.1). Put any follow-up call in the calendar before hanging up. Accept a clean no and ask: "Wen würdest du an meiner Stelle stattdessen fragen?"

## 5.9 Debrief capture (within 2 h, into interactions/)
Their exact phrases (pain, objections, language that resonated), commitments table (who/what/condition/date), named people offered and permission level, what claim needed checking, which objection came up, one learning for the next call of this type. Separate observed facts from interpretation; mark transcript-derived quotes as unreliable (as both existing debriefs correctly do).

# 6. Legal, platform and ethics risks (risk guidance, not legal advice)
- **Recording/transcription:** §201 StGB + DSGVO: get explicit, prior, informed consent before recording or using an AI notetaker (Zoom/Teams/Otter etc.); otherwise only handwritten/typed notes. Note consent in the brief. Do not share transcripts with third parties.
- **Notes on people (DSGVO):** keep debriefs factual, minimal and private; no sensitive data (political opinions of the contact are special-category data under Art. 9 — record professional context, not personal political views).
- **Email after the call (UWG §7):** a recap or requested material is fine; adding the contact to a newsletter or sending unsolicited promotional mail to others named in the call is not. Intros only via double opt-in.
- **Press (Pressekodex, Ziff. 1/2/6/7; it binds journalists, not sources, so this is about not putting them in a conflict):** never ask for coverage in exchange for anything; no exclusives promised to several outlets; numbers must be checkable; Autorisierung covers quotes, not the article; agree on/off-the-record before saying it.
- **Creators (Werbekennzeichnung, MStV/UWG, Medienanstalten guideline):** if any Gegenleistung flows (money, goods, services), the content must be labelled; if nothing flows, do not label it "Werbung" (Medienanstalten advise against inaccurate labels); document that nothing was paid ([Medienanstalten](https://www.die-medienanstalten.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/praktisch-und-bewaehrt-medienanstalten-aktualisieren-leitfaden-werbekennzeichung-bei-online-medien/); [Wettbewerbszentrale 2024-08](https://www.wettbewerbszentrale.de/werbekennzeichnung-beim-influencer-marketing-wettbewerbszentrale-veroeffentlicht-leitfaden/)).
- **Funders / Gemeinnützigkeit:** political neutrality matters for gemeinnützige Träger [PRUEFEN with WE AID: what BnB may say about the support and political topics]; never claim impact you cannot measure; respect stated application processes instead of lobbying staff.
- **Honesty:** no invented traction, quotes or relationships; name-dropping a connector only with explicit permission (e.g. a connector contact allowed it for two named people).

# 6a. Added in review: three missing points that move replies and leads

1. **Let the other person state the next step as when/where/who, then write it into the recap.** "Ich schau mal" rarely happens; "Ich zeig die Seite am Dienstag im Kampagnen-Call unserer Leiterin" often does. Close question: "Wann und wo würdest du das konkret machen?" Then repeat their sentence in the recap (template D). Evidence: implementation intentions (if-then plans with when/where) show a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment across many studies (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38, 69-119) [PRUEFEN: effect size and study count not re-fetched in this review, paywalled]. *verified-source for the general effect; transfer to a multiplier's promise is inference.* Change: section 5.8 asks for when/where, not just a date Thomas proposes.
2. **Leave every call with a ready-to-use asset in *their* format, sent the same day, so their next action takes under 5 minutes.** Journalist: one-page fact sheet with every number sourced and dated (what is founder-reported vs checked), plus whether a letter writer would talk to them. Creator: the test link plus 2-3 sentences they can rewrite in their own words, no script. NGO: the prefilled campaign page. Funder: nothing until they invited the Skizze. Connector: template C. Evidence: journalists reject pitches with missing or unverifiable sources (33%) and want verified sources and third-party data (Muck Rack 2025 via PR News, re-checked); creators insist on creative control (CreatorIQ 2025). *verified-source + inference.* This is where a call turns into a share, an article or a campaign.
3. **End with the "own words" test and capture the sentence.** Last question before the close: "Wie würdest du Brief nach Berlin in einem Satz jemandem erklären?" It shows whether the call landed, and their sentence is the best raw material for the blurb, LinkedIn posts and Reel hooks (with permission before quoting them publicly). Evidence: Fitzpatrick's Mom Test advice to collect the other person's own words over one's pitch language ([mtlynch.io summary](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)). *practitioner-consensus + inference.* Plus, for low-energy days: a **3-line minimum brief** (target commitment + date, one [SIGNAL], one demo moment tested) beats skipping prep; the full 5.2 brief is for journalist and funder calls.
