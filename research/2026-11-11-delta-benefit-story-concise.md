# Delta: benefit-first, emotional story, concise wording

Scope: what is new, better sourced, or contradicting versus `human-writing.md`, `short-punchy-content.md`, `audience-language.md`, `conversation-principles.md`, `first-touch.md` (Drafting card), `buyers-users.md`, `2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, `2026-10-01-linkedin-writing.md`, and the register. Not repeated here: Gong 28M/304k/85M figures, Lavender, Instantly, Muck Rack/Propel length, the BIT EAST framing.

Headline for the founder's two complaints. (1) Line two should say what the recipient gets: supported by field evidence that making the concrete benefit salient and the wording simple raises action (claim 2), but no randomized test of benefit-first versus feature-first outreach copy exists in what I found; it stays a working rule. (2) "More emotional": no source shows that more emotion raises replies from professional recipients. The only reply-level data (vendor, claim 11) favour mild warmth, not intensity, and the one study with legislators shows a story can backfire (claim 3).

Method note: "Read as of" says what I actually opened on 2026-11-11. Where the publisher blocked the page (HTTP 403), I read the abstract through Crossref/OpenAlex metadata and say so. Full texts were not read unless stated.

## Contradicts or weakens existing guidance

- `knowledge/foundations/short-punchy-content.md`, section "Emotion and evidence": "Emotion opens attention. Evidence earns trust." is stated as fact with no source. Claims 5, 8 and 10 show evidence adds little on top of a story for donors and can matter more than feeling for professionals; claim 3 shows a story can raise counterarguing. Treat it as a working rule, not a finding.
- `knowledge/foundations/short-punchy-content.md`, section "Core shape" (Scene, Tension, Lesson): the narrative evidence (claims 3, 4) is mass-audience and average-effect; it supports a true scene as an option for a public post, and the repo already says it is optional. It does not support a scene in a private first touch to a decision-maker.
- `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`, Drafting card, GOOD NGO example: line two describes how the tool works ("geben PLZ und Anliegen ein und bekommen ... einen Briefentwurf ..."). That is the exact pattern the founder complains about and conflicts with the card's own rule "not how the tool works". The example is the cause of the drift, not the founder's drafts alone. No external source is needed for this internal inconsistency.
- `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`, Drafting card, "Line 2, their benefit": the rule is sound but has no randomized support for outreach copy (see "Not found"). The register should label it a working rule; claims 2 and 7 give indirect support only.
- `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`, Kurz-Modus (about 40 words): not contradicted, but claim 11 (vendor, 2016) reports a steeper reply decline below 50 words, while Lavender (25 to 50) is already cited. The evidence on the floor is conflicting and all observational; do not treat 40 as proven optimal.
- `knowledge/evidence/claims-register.md`, row "Pitches under 150-200 words ... ~4x": claim 1 gives an independent randomized datapoint for the direction (not for the 4x figure or for journalists). The vendor row can stay unverified; add the new row below.
- `knowledge/foundations/audience-language.md` / `human-writing.md` on register mirroring: not contradicted. Claim 12 only warns against assuming a casual register helps when the sender is unknown.

## Not found or unverified

- No randomized test of benefit-first versus feature-first copy in cold email or DMs; only observational vendor data (Gong executive piece, Prospeo 64,562 emails with unnamed source, BusySeed 287,790 emails where a percentage figure had the lowest reply rate at 0.8% versus 1.3% no proof; not randomly assigned). The line-two rule stays a working rule.
- No study on emotional appeals versus plain facts for journalists, funders, foundation programme officers or NGO staff. Press-pitch sources found are PR-industry surveys (relevance and over-promotion are the main rejection reasons), already summarised in `research/2026-10-10-press-pitch-benchmarks.md`.
- Reply-level evidence for "felt problem first, then benefit": none found. Gain versus loss framing: no outreach evidence; health-message meta-analyses only.
- Oppenheimer 2006 (needless long words lower perceived intelligence): single study, no direct replication found, original not opened; not used in the table.
- Cryder, Loewenstein and Scheines 2013 (tangible details raise giving, OBHDP): found and not opened beyond a search summary; no replication found. Berger and Milkman 2012 (high-arousal emotion and sharing of NYT articles): abstract read, observational on news articles and about sharing, not replies; not used in the table.
- Full texts not read: Braddock and Dillard, Green and Brock, van Laer, Freling, Lee and Feeley, Maier et al., Packard and Berger, Karlan and Wood, Chen et al., Gallagher and Updegraff, Linos et al. Effect sizes for van Laer and Freling not seen.
- Maier et al. publication year: Collabra vol. 9 (2023 article number 90203), Crossref lists issue year 2024; the JDM 2024 Kogut and Ritov replication was seen only in a search summary.
- No German-language field data on any of the above. All transfer to German outreach is assumption.
