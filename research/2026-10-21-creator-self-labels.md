# Creator self-labels: one creator's essay on the word "Influencer"

**Source:** Bob Blume, "Ich bin selbst Influencer. Glaubt mir nicht alles.", newsletter *Stimme für Bildungswandel* (Substack), 2026-10-17. Supplied by the founder as a saved web page; post URL not captured. Same author as `research/2026-10-07-creator-reach-practitioner-report.md`. The essay is mainly a media-literacy lesson plan for schools; only the opening section bears on outreach. Paraphrased below; no outcome data.

## Findings (paraphrased)

1. **He avoids the label he coined.** His publisher's subtitle called him "Bildungsinfluencer" in 2022; he had it removed from the 2025 paperback and avoids the word wherever he can. He would rather be seen as an education commentator ("Bildungspublizist") who analyses education across channels. He accepts the label only reluctantly when a TV format insists.
2. **His objection is the focus on reach.** The word puts reach first, regardless of whether someone mainly sells products, expertise or information.
3. **Others reject it too.** He reports that a children's TV presenter with his own social channels said in a joint podcast episode that he does not see himself as an education influencer. Second-hand, one example.
4. **Education creators are a mixed group.** He lists very different types (copied or translated US teacher content, trend-riders, personal "yapping", scientists as talking heads, classroom content, activists, mixes). His own line: what counts is a recognisable message built on substantive work, not virality.

## Fit with existing guidance

- **Confirms:** name the specific piece of their work, no follower-count mentions (`knowledge/personas/creators-influencers.md`).
- **Changes:** add one address rule: use the label the person uses for themselves, never "Influencer" or "Influencer-Kooperation" in the message.
- **Not adopted:** the creator typology as a scoring scheme; it is one author's description, not a segmentation with outcomes.
