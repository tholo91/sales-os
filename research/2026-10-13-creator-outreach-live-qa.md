# Creator outreach without reach: live Q&A follow-up

**Source:** Bob Blume, INSIDE live Q&A for paid subscribers of the newsletter *Stimme für Bildungswandel*, 2026-10-13. Not public; an automatic transcript was supplied by the founder (transcription errors, only the creator's answers captured). It follows up on `research/2026-10-07-creator-reach-practitioner-report.md` and answers three subscriber questions. Paraphrased below; one anecdote the author asked listeners not to pass on is left out.

## Findings (paraphrased)

1. **The ask is an application.** A cold ask to a large creator works like a good job application: show that you understood what they make and say why exactly this person fits your project. Generic "please share my passion project" messages lose him immediately. Example from his own past: a well-known YouTuber (Rezo) came into his class unpaid, very likely also because he had read a detailed blog post in which the teacher explained why the YouTuber's video works as teaching material.
2. **Fit before reach.** Reach is not the same as large reach. Very large accounts with a broad audience may not help a niche project at all; follower count alone is meaningless. For awareness he would rather contact 100 small creators with about 1,000 followers each than one with 100,000, each one with real interest in their content. That is work.
3. **Need before reach.** For a product aimed at schools, the first question is not how to get reach but whether the product fits what schools need: go to schools in person and ask what they need and when they would use it, instead of presenting the product. School products often solve a problem the schools do not have or do not think they have.
4. **Warm-up is strategy, and that is fine when the interest is real.** Following and commenting for months is calculated in a sense, but it is the opposite of fake praise. It raises the chance, guarantees nothing. Preparation shows seriousness; a creator should not feel used as a dumping ground for someone else's material.
5. **Persistence needs a prior agreement.** Senders underestimate the creator's inbox: a constant flow of questions, comments and insults, so a missing reply is not a verdict. Without any reply, ask again once, friendly and as well prepared as the first message. Once someone has signalled interest or said "remind me", reminding them is expected and does not annoy, even over a long time: a prominent talk guest took him a year because the guest wanted to but dates kept failing. Stay transparent and keep acknowledging their work. If unsure whether it bothers them, ask.
6. **Acknowledgment, not servility.** Recognise that reach and presence are the result of years of hard work, without being devoted or flattering.
7. **Management and small budgets.** Be transparent and open about a small budget, also with management. Whether he works unpaid or for little depends on many factors; effort weighs heavily (a talk that costs three travel days is hard even when it pays). Do not expect the management of a top celebrity to answer a small project.
8. **Always ask, but not by volume.** He gets turned down a lot and still asks; a no costs nothing, then try the next one. He explicitly rejects mass outreach (thousands of messages). A creator-search tool that filters by creator type, reach and topic can help find candidates; then contact them or their management individually and respectfully. The tool name is unclear in the transcript.

## Fit with existing guidance

- **Confirms:** no generic or mass messages, warm-up as a raised chance not a guarantee, silence is load not rejection, one friendly F1 without an answer, management above a size threshold, reach as the result of work, the Treppe.
- **Changes:** the "why you, specifically" fit argument and a made artifact about their work as the strongest proof; audience fit over reach for awareness; problem validation before creator outreach; invited reminders after a yes without a date; transparency about budget via management; acknowledgment without servility.
- **Fit with the core touch rules:** the persistence finding is compatible with `core/steps/reply.md` (an invited delay keeps its timing) and `core/steps/continue.md` (a missed commitment is not a debt): reminders stay invited touches, each tied to a named or newly given timing, never a cold chase.
- **Not adopted:** the 100-versus-1 ratio as a fixed rule; any unnamed tool as a recommendation; bulk shortlisting or contacting (guardrail: no scraping, bulk enrichment or automatic contact). The Treppe and the persistence heuristic stay quarantined until tracked outcomes exist.
