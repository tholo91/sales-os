# Example: Niraj Pant's Snapchat internship email

The founder supplied this as a successful US cold-email example. The outcome is reported, not independently verified here. It is a reference example for short, direct anglo-American outreach, not a performance benchmark or a send-ready template.

The surrounding inbox text (`me Apr 14, 2014`, recipient, and ellipses) is omitted. Punctuation and bullet formatting are lightly normalized.

```text
Hey, my name's Niraj Pant.

I understand your time is valuable. I'll only write three bullet points.

- Programming since 8th grade.
- Have most experience working in Java/Obj-C/Android/iOS.
- Want to intern for Snapchat this summer as a high school junior.

How?

Thanks,
Niraj
```

## Why this is useful

- It acknowledges the recipient's limited attention and sets a strict reading budget.
- It compresses the sender's relevant background and objective into three concrete lines.
- It states the desired outcome directly instead of hiding it behind a long introduction.
- It ends with one unusually short, easy-to-understand question.
- It avoids generic praise, product exposition, multiple asks, and manufactured urgency.

## What transfers to founder outreach

For an anglo-American recipient, a compact structure can work when the relevance is immediately clear:

1. Say who you are.
2. Give up to three pieces of relevant proof or context.
3. State what you want.
4. Ask one direct question.

For product outreach, replace the generic credential list with a verified target-specific observation or one honest reason this person is relevant. Do not copy the three-bullet format mechanically, invent proof, or assume that US directness is appropriate for every person or channel.

This example complements the evidence-first structure in `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`; it does not replace the requirement for a real target, truthful relevance, and one small ask.
