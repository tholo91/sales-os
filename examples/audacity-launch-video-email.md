# Example: audacity launch-video cold email

The founder supplied this as a cheeky US-tech cold-email example that turns the template-reload objection into an attention hook. The origin is an online inbox example; the senders and outcomes are not verified here. It is a reference example for tone and structure, not a performance benchmark or a send-ready template.

The founder's annotations describe the mechanics: the sender makes launch videos, the message was marked "(Edited)", the subject line doubles as the first line, and a founder-style signature ("Thomas, founder of ...") would stand at the end.

```text
Subject: Let me help Agent37

Hi Vishnu,

I know what you are thinking. You are thinking: "did this lazy piece of sh!t actually forget to fill in the template?" Or something along those lines.

Well, I would like to inform you of 2 things, Vishnu:
1) I am actually typing this out
2) I make launch videos and want to make one for Agent37

Are you looking for a launch video?

[signature - "Thomas, founder of ..."]
```

## Why this is useful

- It names the exact suspicion a savvy tech recipient has on first sight ("forgot to fill in the template") and pre-empts it instead of ignoring it.
- The numbered two-item structure sets a strict reading budget: two facts, then one question.
- The subject line is a plain offer, not a tease.
- The closing question is close-ended and trivially easy to answer.

## What transfers to founder outreach

1. Acknowledge the recipient's likely reaction honestly before arguing for attention.
2. Keep a strict, short body: at most two relevant facts and one direct question.
3. Tie the offer to one concrete deliverable in the recipient's context (a launch video for their product), not to your full offering.

## What does not transfer without care

- The audacity only disarms when the facts are truthful and the relevance is real; as a copied pattern it becomes recognizable noise.
- The self-censored profanity is not a safe default for products or recipients outside a specific anglo-American tech culture.
- The subject-in-body and founder-signature mechanics still require a real target, verified language evidence, and truthful relevance (see `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`).

This example complements the evidence-first structure in `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`; it does not replace the requirement for a real target, truthful relevance, and one small ask.
