# Example: DataFast "data will be deleted" reactivation email

A real third-party paid-trial reactivation email (pasted from the founder's received `.eml`). Not framework truth, not Sales OS copy, not send-ready for any project.

Source: received email from DataFast (datafa.st), sent via Resend. Quoted as received (plain-text part).

Context: The recipient ended a free trial and did not pick a plan. DataFast is a bootstrapped paid analytics tool where free users generate real cost. This is not a cold first touch and not generic marketing — it is a transactional email about a real consequence.

```
Hey there,

I noticed you haven't picked a plan after your free trial ended. I assume
DataFast might not be the right fit for your needs right now, which is totally
fine!

As a bootstrapped founder without VC funding, I need to keep costs low to
maintain fair pricing for everyone. This means in 48 hours, DataFast will stop
tracking new events on your websites and your existing analytics data will be
deleted.

If you'd still like to keep your data and continue using DataFast, you can pick
a plan here [https://datafast.st/dashboard/billing].

And if you have any questions or feedback, I'm here to help - just reply to this
email.

Best,
Marc
```

## Why this works

- Names the real state of the account ("haven't picked a plan after your free trial ended") instead of a vague "we noticed you left".
- Gives the recipient an easy, respectful out first ("might not be the right fit — totally fine"), so the email reads as honest, not pushy.
- The urgency is real and explained: a genuine cost constraint (bootstrapped founder, free-tier cost), translated into a concrete consequence with a specific deadline ("in 48 hours") and a specific effect on the recipient's own data ("tracking stops, data deleted").
- One clear CTA that preserves the recipient's interest ("pick a plan here"), plus a human, low-pressure exit ("just reply to this email").
- Signed by a human name from a real person mailbox (`marc@resend.datafa.st` with `Reply-To: marc@datafa.st`), not a no-reply address.

## Best use: talking to people who want to quit or won't pay

The main lesson is tone for churn/no-pay situations, not automation:

- Read the unspoken reason yourself and name it kindly ("might not be the right fit right now"), so the recipient doesn't need to defend the decision or feel guilt-tripped into staying.
- Never beg or manufacture pressure. If there is a real consequence of staying on the current terms, state it factually and explain why it exists; if there isn't, don't invent one.
- Leave a door open that respects the decision ("totally fine") while offering one action for whoever still wants it.
- End human — invite a reply, don't end with a deadline.

## What does NOT transfer to Sales OS outreach

Sales OS deliberately avoids automated follow-up sequences, and `handle-follow-up` forbids invented urgency and generic cadence. This email only works because the 48-hour deadline is a factual product consequence, not a manufactured discount countdown. Copying the shape without that real trigger would be exactly the kind of fake urgency Sales OS guards against.

## What transfers to founder-led outreach

- Real consequences, stated specifically, beat generic urgency. Only use a deadline when a real, recipient-relevant consequence exists (their data, a real cost, a real removal) and explain why it exists.
- State the account/relationship fact precisely rather than pushing a pitch.
- Give one clear action that serves the recipient's interest, and a real human to reply to.
