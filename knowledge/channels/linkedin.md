# LinkedIn

## Best use

Use visible professional context, warm paths, and public micro-engagement to earn a relevant private conversation.

Do not optimise for virality. LinkedIn distributes content through relevance signals spanning profile, network, content, activity, recency, and constructive-professional conversation. A small, relevant network can therefore support a useful post; a big audience is not a prerequisite for a meaningful test.

The operating goal is zero blank-page work, not autonomous account activity. Sales OS may research public context and prepare a finished draft, but the founder reviews and performs every post, comment, invitation, and message.

## Sequence

1. Verify the person's current role and a real public trigger. Start with existing 1st-degree contacts who show a fitting signal.
2. Leave a useful public comment 1–7 days before (`warm-contact-comment`), or use a warm introduction.
3. Send the connection request without a note by default: free accounts get only a few notes a month (limit in `knowledge/markets/germany-eu.md`, section `## Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach`; account type `[PRÜFEN: Free oder Premium?]`). Save notes for top multipliers; never pitch in a note.
4. Send the first DM within a day of acceptance: one observation about their work or action moment, what their people get, and one easy question from the ask ladder in `knowledge/strategies/first-touch.md`. Permission to send an example is allowed; a call only with concrete context.
5. Follow up per the touch plan in `knowledge/strategies/follow-up.md`. A 1st-degree voice note (mobile only, 60 seconds or less) is allowed as F2 for warm-ish contacts, never as the opener.

Messaging someone who is not a 1st-degree connection generally needs InMail; do not treat a public comment as a DM route. Legal and platform limits: `knowledge/markets/germany-eu.md` (`## Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach`).

## Native writing shape

- Use the founder's true identity and relationship to the product; never simulate a team or borrowed authority.
- Use one verified professional hook. Generic praise is not a hook.
- Do not pitch in the invitation note.
- Keep a first private message to one context and one useful point, ending with exactly one easy question from the ask ladder.
- Final copy must match the founder's own channel and language evidence. Platform-style “professional” polish does not outrank founder voice.
- Follow the person's visible `du`/`Sie` and formality signals; do not decide them from the German market file alone.
- Treat generated copy as a first draft until the founder has checked every fact, added a real point of view, and confirmed it sounds like him. LinkedIn gives the same instruction for its own AI writing feature.

## Founder-led practice

- A message from the founder who built the product carries more credibility than delegated outreach; write as the builder, name the founder role, and never simulate a sales team.
- Engage genuinely with the person's recent public content before connecting, when it exists; a specific comment earns more than any connection-note formula.
- The optional connection note: reference one specific, real thing (their post, talk, role change, shared context). Do not pitch or ask for a separate favor in it.
- First DM after connecting: thank briefly when natural, reference the specific context, explain why you are writing, and offer one useful observation or small question about their work — no product pitch, calendar link, attachment, introduction, or share request by default; a meeting only per ask-ladder step 3.
- Treat a substantive reply as engagement. Accepting a connection or liking a post alone does not justify a larger ask; first respond to what the person actually said.
- Reply-rate and acceptance-rate percentages circulating in outreach blogs are unsourced marketing claims; per the source policy they stay out of this file.
- A public post should make its reader benefit clear through a concrete lesson, observation, question, or relevant announcement. LinkedIn recommends useful content and genuine engagement; do not treat that as a guarantee of reach or hide an explicit product announcement inside a forced personal story.
- Prefer a clear point of view, a specific work scene or proof point, and one useful takeaway. The official sharing guide recommends conversation, timely expertise, genuine replies, and visual context rather than promotional repetition.
- For native video, hook quickly, film vertically, make the subject audible, add on-screen context, and use the official 30-second-to-two-minute range only as a starting guide.

## Profile as landing page

- The headline names the project and what it does in plain words; keep it consistent with the post topics.
- Featured holds the site, the organisation or campaign page, and the best explainer post; About ends with who should write about what.
- Curious readers and DM recipients check the profile before the website; LinkedIn ranks with profile identity signals ([LinkedIn Help a1339724](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724), checked 2026-10-04).

## Public posts

For drafting, editing, media choices, and performance review, read `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md` (sources checked 2026-10-01). It distinguishes personal stories, evidence-led insights, and explicit announcements; a scene and CTA are optional. Its dated platform evidence updates public-post guidance without changing the invitation and DM sequence above.

## Content before outreach

The 2025 Edelman–LinkedIn study found that management-level buyers use high-quality thought leadership to assess vendors and become more receptive to outreach. Its useful direction is qualitative: reveal a challenge the buyer may have missed, challenge an assumption, stay human, and avoid turning the post into a product sheet. The survey is US-based and self-reported; never convert its percentages into expected reach, replies, or sales.

Current research reference: [2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2025-06/2025%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report_FINAL.pdf) (verified 2026-08-21).

## Guardrails

- No fake familiarity, generic praise, or invented relationship.
- No scraping, automated account access, automated invitations, messages, posting, commenting, reactions, or engagement.
- No universal daily invitation limits in the knowledge base; platform behaviour and enforcement change. The 3-notes-a-month limit above is a documented feature limit, not an invitation cap.
- Human review and action are mandatory.
