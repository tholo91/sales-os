# Germany and EU

## Communication hypothesis

When recipient evidence is thin, start with a factual reason for contact, a concrete benefit or question, and one easy-to-decline next step. This is an unproven writing default, not a finding that German people prefer one style or that it increases replies. Role, relationship, current wording, and channel evidence take precedence.

This file cannot decide `du`/`Sie`, humor, greeting, sign-off, or directness. Use the person's wording, visible professional context, relationship history, and exact channel norms first. When evidence conflicts, choose the more respectful form or ask rather than pretending confidence.

## Risk boundary

Classify the actual purpose first: commercial promotion, civic engagement, an editorial story, genuine research, or an agreed operational reply. Under [UWG section 2](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__2.html), the relevant business-action definition concerns conduct objectively linked to promoting goods, services, or contracts. A genuinely noncommercial civic exchange may have a different analysis from a sales pitch. A free product, nonprofit label, request for feedback, or journalist recipient does not by itself settle that classification. Do not disguise promotion as research or assume a public editorial address permits unrelated product promotion.

Promotional cold email and telephone outreach can be legally sensitive. Review the exact channel, relationship, audience, source of contact data, and current law before acting. Prefer warm introductions, public participation, explicit feedback requests, and expected professional contact paths where appropriate.

For electronic mail, UWG section 7 generally requires prior express consent for consumers and other market participants. The existing-customer exception is usable only when all statutory conditions are documented. For B2B phone calls, presumed consent has a different and still demanding test; do not transfer it to email or treat a public phone number, website visit, funding event, LinkedIn interaction, or job title as sufficient by itself.

GDPR and UWG answer different questions. A GDPR processing basis does not make a marketing channel permissible. When personal data is processed for direct marketing, Article 21 gives the person a right to object at any time and requires that right to be highlighted by the first communication. If data came from a public or third-party source, assess the Article 14 information duty as well.

The ePrivacy definition of electronic mail is broad enough that commercial social-network DMs should not be presumed exempt. When the exact channel classification matters, seek qualified current advice instead of encoding a loophole.

## Required risk facts before sending

- Sender and recipient jurisdictions.
- Consumer or business context and the actual recipient role.
- Relationship and prior interactions.
- Source and date of the contact detail.
- Consent, existing-customer basis, expected-contact evidence, or other claimed permission.
- Prior objection, unsubscribe, or community rule.
- Exact channel, commercial purpose, sender identity, and manual next action.

Draft first and mark a missing fact `[PRÜFEN: …]` per `knowledge/foundations/human-writing.md`; resolve it before sending. Reuse known facts rather than asking the founder for a checklist every time. An accepted LinkedIn connection is relationship evidence, not general consent to advertising. A follow-up requires a permitted channel too; silence does not expand consent. A requested answer stays within the recipient's actual request.

If a missing purpose, permission, or jurisdiction fact materially changes channel suitability, research that fact before finalizing the affected message. Continue independent recipient research and offer an available warm or public route. A cold promotional request for consent is not automatically a lawful workaround. Better copy is not a compliance fix.

Primary legal starting points: [UWG section 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html), [GDPR Articles 3, 6, 14 and 21](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), and [ePrivacy Article 2(h)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32002L0058). Germany's UWG rules are not a complete guide to every EU country's national implementation. Evidence and access limits from the 2026-10-02 check are in `research/2026-10-02-germany-us-outreach.md`. This is risk guidance, not a case-specific legal determination.

## Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach

The single compact reference; other files link here instead of restating. Risk guidance checked 2026-10-04, not legal advice. Sources: `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md`, `research/2026-10-04-persona-creators-influencers.md`, `research/2026-10-04-instagram-reels-hooks.md`, `research/2026-10-04-linkedin-posts-2026-delta.md`.

- **Email, UWG §7:** cold email only as an editorial pitch with a real story to an editorial address, or via a published partner, submission, or programme route (contact form, call, application portal). No info@ blasts, no cold email sequences. A free or nonprofit tool does not make a cold promotional email safe, and offering anything in return (visibility, logo, mention) makes it look more like advertising.
- **Creators, §5a Abs. 4 UWG and Werbekennzeichnung:** a genuinely unpaid share without strings normally needs no label, but consideration is presumed unless the creator can show otherwise. No money, perks, tickets, travel, merch, or reciprocal promotion; no scripted copy. After a yes, state in writing `kein Geld, keine Gegenleistung, keine Textvorgaben`. Do not suggest "unbezahlte Werbung" labels. Paid political advertising in video is not allowed ([Medienanstalten guideline](https://www.die-medienanstalten.de/service/merkblaetter-und-leitfaeden/leitfaden-werbekennzeichnung-bei-online-medien/)); whether a nonpartisan civic tool counts as political advertising is open.
- **Press, Pressekodex (Ziffer 7 and 15):** keep editorial and promotion separate. Offer no benefit for coverage, do not ask for favourable framing, article approval, or a donation link; offer verifiable data.
- **Data, GDPR Art. 9:** letter topics can reveal political opinions. Publish only opt-in aggregates with a minimum cell size, with definition, date, and n; an individual case only with that person's explicit consent. Art. 14 and 21 apply as above.
- **Meta ads:** since 2025-10-06 Meta runs no social-issue, electoral, or political ads in the EU (TTPA, Regulation 2024/900). Organic posts are unaffected; do not plan boosts or paid creator ads.
- **LinkedIn invitations:** free accounts can add a personalized note to 3 connection requests a month, 200 characters maximum ([Help a563153](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153)); Premium removes the cap. Invitations must not be promotional; a withdrawn invitation cannot be resent for up to three weeks ([Help a551012](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012)).
- **No engagement automation:** no bots, scraping, auto-DMs, comment-keyword DM tools, pre-agreed like or share swaps, or engagement-bait CTAs ("Kommentiere X") on LinkedIn or Instagram. Every message is individual and sent by the founder.
- **Platform DM limits:** no uninvited Reddit DM. On Instagram a non-follower gets one text-only message request until they accept (reported 2023-08, adult applicability `[PRÜFEN: …]`), so no second DM into an unaccepted request and no link or clip in DM 1. Use the public reply, a published business email, or the stated inquiry route instead.
