# Germany and EU

## Communication hypothesis

Default to calm, specific language with fewer unsupported claims. Explain why the recipient is relevant and make the ask easy to decline. Treat this as a starting hypothesis, not a stereotype; match the actual person and relationship.

This file cannot decide `du`/`Sie`, humor, greeting, sign-off, or directness. Use the person's wording, visible professional context, relationship history, and exact channel norms first. When evidence conflicts, choose the more respectful form or ask rather than pretending confidence.

## Risk boundary

Promotional cold email and telephone outreach can be legally sensitive. Review the exact channel, relationship, audience, source of contact data, and current law before acting. Prefer warm introductions, public participation, explicit feedback requests, and expected professional contact paths where appropriate.

For electronic mail, UWG section 7 generally requires prior express consent for consumers and other market participants. The existing-customer exception is usable only when all statutory conditions are documented. For B2B phone calls, presumed consent has a different and still demanding test; do not transfer it to email or treat a public phone number, website visit, funding event, LinkedIn interaction, or job title as sufficient by itself.

GDPR and UWG answer different questions. A GDPR processing basis does not make a marketing channel permissible. When personal data is processed for direct marketing, Article 21 gives the person a right to object at any time and requires that right to be highlighted by the first communication. If data came from a public or third-party source, assess the Article 14 information duty as well.

The ePrivacy definition of electronic mail is broad enough that commercial social-network DMs should not be presumed exempt. When the exact channel classification matters, seek qualified current advice instead of encoding a loophole.

## Required risk facts before a draft

- Sender and recipient jurisdictions.
- Consumer or business context and the actual recipient role.
- Relationship and prior interactions.
- Source and date of the contact detail.
- Consent, existing-customer basis, expected-contact evidence, or other claimed permission.
- Prior objection, unsubscribe, or community rule.
- Exact channel, commercial purpose, sender identity, and manual next action.

If these facts do not support the channel, route to a warm introduction, useful public contribution, permission request through an expected path, or qualified legal review. Better copy is not a compliance fix.
