# Human writing

Human writing is not created by sprinkling casual words into a template. It comes from a real reason to speak, evidence about how this person communicates, and the founder's own habits in that channel.

## Build from evidence

1. Start from the language-evidence packet in `audience-language.md`.
2. Match only the corresponding channel and language section of `workspace/voice.md`.
3. Use the target's nouns, level of detail, and visible formality without mimicking identity, dialect, or private speech.
4. Write one thing the founder honestly wants to say; add one easy question only if it belongs in this exchange.
5. Use only facts and proof recorded in project or target evidence, or found with URL and date in the Draft-first look (rule 2 below).

When evidence is missing, write a shorter draft from verified facts and mark each gap; generic polish is never a substitute for a marker.

## Draft-first and gap markers

Founder decision 2026-10-04. Every drafting skill references this section instead of restating it.

1. Return one usable draft in the same turn for every drafting request: DM, email, comment, post, Reel script, follow-up, reply, call brief, or recap.
2. Draft-first means no refusal, not no research. Before a first touch or follow-up to a real, named person or organisation, when web or read-only browser tools are available, take one bounded look (about five minutes, read-only, public pages only, no login-walled scraping, no bulk) at their current public context: latest posts or articles, current campaign, hearing or episode, beat or programme page. The strongest dated, verifiable item becomes the opener and the reason why now; name its URL and date in the rationale and never imply the founder knew it earlier. Write URL and date into the contact file only after the founder confirms. Without such tools, ask nothing extra and draft with markers.
3. Mark gaps inline at the exact spot, only for what that look could not establish:
   - `[SIGNAL: …]` a person-specific observation the founder fills in from what he actually saw (post plus date, quote, campaign moment).
   - `[PRÜFEN: …]` a claim to verify before sending: number with definition and date, live feature, consent, contact route, deadline.
   - `[ENTSCHEIDUNG: …]` a choice only the founder can make; use it rarely.
   - Register: use the du/Sie recorded for this contact, else the persona or channel default, without an inline marker; only when neither exists does du/Sie become one check item.
4. Neutral fill is allowed: verified role, outlet, and organization facts, recorded project facts, and the persona playbook's generic angle. Never fill a gap with an invented trigger ("dein Post", "wie du in den Kommentaren …"), number, quote, relationship, consent, live-feature claim, or impact claim. A request that only works with an invented fact gets the draft without that fact, plus a marker. Approved proof lines in the project's `## Gemeinsame Belegzeilen`, used in their exact wording with their date, are neutral fill and need no marker. No unmarked generalisation over project history (`lange`, `fast jeder`, `immer`) and no present-tense promise about something unrecorded (e.g. a published methodology).
5. Use the skeleton only when neither the person nor the organisation is identifiable; a named outlet, organisation, desk, or role gets a normal draft with `[SIGNAL: Name]`.
6. After the draft, add one line `Vor dem Senden prüfen:` with at most three items, one check each, send-blockers first; inside the copilot wrapper they go under `Dein manueller Schritt:`. At least one sentence about this recipient or their audience must come from recorded facts or the rule-2 look. If that is impossible, or the body would need more than three markers, still deliver the draft titled `Skelett – nicht sendefertig`, name the one marker to resolve first, and name the cheaper move in one clause (a hook that existing evidence supports, parking the contact, or one question to the founder).
7. A draft with open markers is draft_needs_check and not send-ready. It becomes draft_ready only when no marker is open, and the final specificity check below still applies.
8. Return no draft only when:
   - (a) the request asks to send, post, scrape, bulk-message, automate engagement, or deceive;
   - (b) the recipient refused or objected to contact;
   - (c) a comment or reply would answer content that is entirely unseen (no text, link, screenshot, or founder paraphrase); a founder paraphrase is enough to draft with `[PRÜFEN: genauer Wortlaut]`; otherwise ask for its text or link;
   - (d) channel norms or law rule out the private message (an uninvited Reddit DM, a second DM into an unaccepted Instagram request, a cold promotional email without consent; see `knowledge/markets/germany-eu.md`, section `Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach`); draft the permitted alternative instead (public reply, comment, LinkedIn message, or the stated inquiry route); for a post nobody has seen yet, the permitted alternative is a short comment skeleton with one `[SIGNAL: Kernpunkt des Posts]` slot.

## Output economy

- Put the draft before explanation.
- Produce one draft with at most one ask in a cold first touch. Do not invent a question to fill a template.
- Explain the choice in one short sentence.
- Add a risk note only for a real factual, cultural, platform, legal, or relationship risk.
- Create an alternative only when the founder requests one.
- End with one ask answerable with yes, no, a date, or a name; never an either-or menu, never ask the recipient to choose the format or remember context.
- Cut product history, founder biography, broad trends, and proof that the ask does not need.

## Human shape by channel

- A Reddit comment should read as part of the thread, usually without greeting or sign-off.
- A LinkedIn note needs one real professional hook, not a campaign opener.
- A Slack contribution must fit the workspace's live norms and should be useful without a product.
- An email must establish why this recipient and why this contact route are appropriate before it optimizes wording.
- A reply or follow-up should continue the existing relationship language, not restart the pitch.
- Spoken text (Reels, voice notes, call pitches): short main clauses, one thought per breath, read it aloud, no written-only constructions; the AI-shaped lint below applies to voiceovers too.

Allow contractions, short replies, and small imperfections when the founder evidence supports them and clarity remains. Do not manufacture typos, dialect, emojis, humor, or false familiarity to look human.

## Final specificity check

- Could this message be sent unchanged to another person? If yes, rewrite or cut.
- Does every claim have a source? If no, remove it.
- If there is an ask, does it test the learning goal without imposing substantial work? If no, simplify or remove it.
- Does the message look native in this exact surface? If no, change the shape before polishing words.

## AI-shaped lint, last

Word blacklists are a final lint, not a voice generator. Cut or rewrite these only when they make the sentence abstract or unlike the supported voice:

- delve
- underscore
- showcase
- foster
- garner
- elevate
- navigate
- resonate
- crucial
- pivotal
- vital
- intricate
- vibrant
- groundbreaking
- seamless
- tapestry
- testament
- abstract landscape
- interplay
- synergies
- serves as
- stands as

Also check for formulaic structures:

- "In conclusion", "In summary", or "Overall" openings.
- Optimistic wrap-up endings after mentioning challenges.
- Rule-of-three adjective piles.
- "Not only X, but also Y" constructions.
- Correcting a misconception the recipient did not raise.
- Bullet lists formatted as bold concept labels followed by explanations.
- Contrast slogans (`X bedeutet mir mehr als Y`, `zeigt kein Zähler`): at most one per text, never as the closing line.

Delete reusable compliments, “just wanted to reach out,” automatic “no pressure,” and tidy inspirational endings unless the real context makes them natural.
