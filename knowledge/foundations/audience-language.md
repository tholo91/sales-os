# Audience language

Audience language is evidence from a real person or live community, not a tone guessed from age, country, job title, or buyer persona.

## Minimum evidence packet

Before drafting, collect four small pieces:

1. `Target wording`: a short public excerpt or careful paraphrase of how the person describes the situation now.
2. `Discussion context`: what they were responding to, asking for, or trying to do.
3. `Culture or rule check`: current written rules plus one observed interaction norm from the exact community or platform surface.
4. `Observed tone`: practical traits such as concise/detailed, formal/informal, technical/plain, humorous/serious, or reply/post shape.

Record the permalink or source, date, and whether each item is verified. If a material item is missing, return to research instead of producing a polished guess.

## Collection rules

- Prefer the person's current public words and the live conversation over profile stereotypes.
- Preserve only the minimum excerpt needed to support a language decision. Paraphrase when exact wording is unnecessary.
- Do not copy private Slack messages, DMs, emails, call transcripts, or user histories into public knowledge or examples.
- A word used once is a local signal, not permission to imitate dialect, identity language, slang, or humor.
- Separate language the audience uses to describe the problem from marketing words the product uses to describe itself.
- Observe message shape as well as vocabulary: answer-first comment, short professional reply, detailed technical post, greeting, sign-off, emoji, and formality.
- Check the actual person's register before deciding German `du` or `Sie`. Country and market files cannot make that decision alone.

## Applying the packet

Use evidence in this order:

1. Verified target context and wording.
2. The founder's matching channel and language voice evidence.
3. Current community and platform norms.
4. The individual's visible formality and relationship history.
5. Broad market guidance as a cautious hypothesis.

Do not copy the target's phrasing so closely that it feels performative. Use it to choose plain nouns, verbs, level of detail, and message shape.

## Opportunity record

Each sourced opportunity should include:

- target or permalink;
- verified date and relevance;
- one observed language signal;
- one rule or culture check;
- relationship path;
- missing evidence and risk;
- one useful human move.

Platform sources emphasize contextual, useful, non-spam participation and genuine professional identity.
