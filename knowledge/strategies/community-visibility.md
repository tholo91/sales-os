# Community visibility without a big audience

Do not make “go viral” the operating goal. It is an unpredictable distribution outcome, not a repeatable tactic. The useful goal is to earn a second conversation, a saved post, a relevant reply, or an introduction from the right people.

You do not need to be famous. LinkedIn says posts are initially shared with a subset of connections and followers, while its feed uses many identity, content, activity, recency, network, and constructive-conversation signals. That means a small, relevant network and a specific point of view can be enough for a useful first test; a large follower count is helpful distribution, not a substitute for relevance. Reddit’s own guidance also says timing, quality, relevance, and luck all matter.

## Prerequisites

Before publishing, have all five:

1. **A real audience surface.** Name the community, the people in it, its rules, and why this topic belongs there.
2. **An earned observation.** Use something seen, built, tried, or learned. Do not manufacture a hot take, customer proof, or market statistic.
3. **One stand-alone payoff.** The post must help even if nobody clicks, buys, or knows the product.
4. **A truthful founder position.** Disclose the relationship to the product when it matters; do not simulate an impartial member or a larger team.
5. **A conversation plan.** Be available to answer substantive replies. Do not post-and-run, buy engagement, coordinate votes, or automate replies.

## A low-key founder pattern

Use a small, credible claim rather than an announcement:

1. Start with a specific situation or decision a peer will recognize.
2. Show the evidence, failed attempt, trade-off, or useful artifact.
3. State the narrow lesson; avoid declaring a universal truth.
4. Ask one question that someone with relevant experience can answer.
5. Reply in the same useful tone, including when the response is critical.

Good: “We tested three ways to ask customers about [workflow]. The only version that produced concrete examples was [approach]. What wording has worked for you?”

Weak: “I built the future of [category] — thoughts?”

The first is useful if it reaches 30 right people. The second needs borrowed attention and usually produces shallow engagement.

## What to measure

Track a dated, platform-specific experiment: surface, audience hypothesis, post premise, replies from relevant people, meaningful follow-ups, and one learning. Views, likes, upvotes, and follower growth are diagnostic signals, not validation. A post with modest reach that opens two useful conversations beats one with broad but irrelevant attention.

Stop or change the premise when replies show confusion, the useful point cannot be stated without a product pitch, or the audience does not recognise the problem. Do not compensate with higher frequency.
