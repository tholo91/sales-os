# Editorial pitch

Use this only for a real journalist, editor, producer, or editorial contact whose current beat or work creates a defensible fit. It is a founder-led story pitch, not a generic sales email and not permission to contact someone.

## Find the story before writing

Write one sentence answering: **What could this outlet truthfully publish as the headline?**

The answer must contain the actual news angle: a current event, verified number, documented case, local pattern, or timely change. The existence of a product is rarely the story. If the angle depends on a metric, comparison, outcome, or causal claim that is not verified, draft with the claim source-labelled (e.g. `laut eigener Zählung, Stand [Datum]`) and marked `[PRÜFEN: Zahl, Definition, Stand]`; verify before sending, not before drafting.

## Subject line

Treat the subject as a candidate headline:

- state the news, not `Introduction`, `Press release`, `Story idea`, or only the product name;
- make the relevance understandable without opening the email;
- keep it truthful and specific rather than clever, inflated, or click-driven;
- match the angle to this outlet or beat rather than using one universal subject.

The subject earns the open; the body must carry the same promise. If the body changes topic or expands into the whole product history, the pitch is not ready.

## Second-day stories

Once an outlet or the national press has covered the product, "this tool exists" is spent. Pitch only a defined data point, a consented protagonist, a local pattern, a verified calendar hook (election, budget, sitting week), a counter-thesis, or an honest failure.

## Numbers

Give every number its definition, date, and n (e.g. drafts created vs. letters confirmed sent). Publish aggregates only: opt-in data with a minimum cell size `[PRÜFEN: Schwelle]`. Never an individual case without that person's explicit consent. Political topics are special-category data; see `knowledge/markets/germany-eu.md` (`## Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach`).

## Reading budget

Keep the body to four functions and remove everything else (120–180 words):

1. **Why this person:** one or two sentences tied to a real article, comment, beat, programme, or local context.
2. **What the news is:** the angle and the minimum verified proof needed to understand why it matters now.
3. **Why this founder can help:** one short sentence naming the founder's real role, access, or first-hand perspective.
4. **What should happen next:** one direct question about editorial interest or a short conversation.

Do not add a general company history, feature tour, several links, several asks, or a press-release block. Put the press-kit link in the signature; link rather than attach (one small photo is acceptable). Match `du` or `Sie` to visible evidence. A scheduling link is optional only when scheduling is already the natural next step; it is not the first-touch CTA by default.

## Founder-led delivery

For a small project, let the founder send individual messages under their real identity. Personalization means one or two relevant sentences based on the recipient's actual work, not a long compliment or synthetic familiarity.

Research can be lightweight but must be real: record the contact route and two short notes about what the person covers before sending; if missing, mark `[PRÜFEN: Ressort / letzter Artikel / Kontaktweg]` in the draft. Public editorial addresses or expected professional routes still require the legal, policy, and relationship checks in `knowledge/channels/email.md`.

## Local and agency desks

Local and regional outlets come first: people from their area, regional numbers, a local face. For a state-level hook, send one pitch to the news agency's regional desk (e.g. the dpa Landesdienst) instead of many papers `[PRÜFEN: aktuelle Adresse des Landesbüros]`. Offer packaged local data to a local-journalism network only when it is clean, aggregated, and dated.

## Follow-up

One follow-up after 4–6 working days in the same thread, adding one new element (a fresh number, an available protagonist, a date hook). Then stop. The next pitch needs a new angle. No "Ist meine Mail angekommen?" call.

## Measure each pitch

Log outlet, segment, angle, reply, publication date, and the effect in the 4 days after publication in the interaction record. After about 10 pitches, keep the segment with the best result per hour. After publication, ask once whether the online version can link the project.

## Test the angle, not cosmetic wording

Send only after manual review and record the outcome. A first directional batch may use 10–15 individually researched contacts. If it produces no substantive reply, reassess the story angle and evidence before changing adjectives, rewriting greetings, or increasing volume.

Treat replies, editorial questions, and conversation invitations as signals. Silence does not prove the product or audience is wrong, but it also does not justify more than the one planned follow-up or a larger batch.
