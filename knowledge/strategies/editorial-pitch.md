# Editorial pitch

Use this only for a real journalist, editor, producer, or editorial contact whose current beat or work creates a defensible fit. It is a founder-led story pitch, not a generic sales email and not permission to contact someone.

## Find the story before writing

Write one sentence answering: **What could this outlet truthfully publish as the headline?**

The answer must contain the actual news angle: a current event, verified number, documented case, local pattern, or timely change. The existence of a product is rarely the story. If the angle depends on a metric, comparison, outcome, or causal claim that is not verified, stop and verify it before drafting.

## Subject line

Treat the subject as a candidate headline:

- state the news, not `Introduction`, `Press release`, `Story idea`, or only the product name;
- make the relevance understandable without opening the email;
- keep it truthful and specific rather than clever, inflated, or click-driven;
- match the angle to this outlet or beat rather than using one universal subject.

The subject earns the open; the body must carry the same promise. If the body changes topic or expands into the whole product history, the pitch is not ready.

## Reading budget

Keep the body to four functions and remove everything else:

1. **Why this person:** one or two sentences tied to a real article, comment, beat, programme, or local context.
2. **What the news is:** the angle and the minimum verified proof needed to understand why it matters now.
3. **Why this founder can help:** one short sentence naming the founder's real role, access, or first-hand perspective.
4. **What should happen next:** one direct question about editorial interest or a short conversation.

Do not add a general company history, feature tour, several links, attachment, several asks, or a press-release block. Match `du` or `Sie` to visible evidence. A scheduling link is optional only when scheduling is already the natural next step; it is not the first-touch CTA by default.

## Founder-led delivery

For a small project, let the founder send individual messages under their real identity. Personalization means one or two relevant sentences based on the recipient's actual work, not a long compliment or synthetic familiarity.

Research can be lightweight but must be real: record the contact route and two short notes about what the person covers before drafting. Public editorial addresses or expected professional routes still require the legal, policy, and relationship checks in `knowledge/channels/email.md`.

## Test the angle, not cosmetic wording

Send only after manual review and record the outcome. A first directional batch may use 10–15 individually researched contacts. If it produces no substantive reply, reassess the story angle and evidence before changing adjectives, rewriting greetings, or increasing volume.

Treat replies, editorial questions, and conversation invitations as signals. Silence does not prove the product or audience is wrong, but it also does not justify automatic follow-up or a larger batch.
