# Objection handling

An objection is information about fit, timing, trust, value, authority, process, or the interruption itself. The goal is to understand and route it, not to defeat the buyer.

## Classify before responding

- **Dismissive:** “Not interested”, “send information”, or another reflexive response to the interruption.
- **Situational:** no budget, wrong timing, missing capacity, procurement, or another current constraint.
- **Current solution:** an incumbent, internal process, or “we already handle this”.
- **Condition:** a genuine boundary that neither side can responsibly change.

Gong and 30MPC analyzed more than 300 million cold calls. Five patterns represented 74% of observed objections, and dismissive objections represented 49.5%. This supports a small objection taxonomy. Their recommended scripts are practitioner guidance layered on the dataset, not separately tested causal evidence. Source: [Gong objection analysis](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all) (verified 2026-08-21).

## Response sequence

1. **Acknowledge:** show that the concern was heard without arguing or apologizing for the offer.
2. **Diagnose:** ask one short question that distinguishes the surface phrase from the real constraint.
3. **Confirm:** summarize the constraint and let the buyer correct it.
4. **Route:** offer one bounded test or next step only if the answer supports one; otherwise stop, defer, or record no-fit.

Examples of diagnostic questions:

- “Is the concern the timing, the investment, or confidence in the outcome?”
- “What works well enough in the current approach that changing it would not be worth the risk?”
- “Who else would need to be comfortable with this?”
- “What would have to change for this to become worth revisiting?”

## Boundaries

- Never invent urgency, a competitor weakness, authority, social proof, or a concession.
- Do not stack rebuttals or questions until the buyer gives in.
- Do not treat a dismissive response as consent to continue a cadence.
- Change price only when scope, timing, risk, or payment shape genuinely changes.
- Record the buyer's wording, the diagnosed constraint, and the resulting decision. An explicit no is useful evidence.
