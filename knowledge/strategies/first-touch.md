# First touch

Body reference: `research/2026-10-04-outbound-first-touch-follow-up.md` (reviewed 2026-10-04).

## Drafting card

A persona's `## Message skeleton` overrides this card's line order, length, and close for that persona (funder sketch, media email pitch); everything else applies.

Use this card first; the sections below explain the boundaries.

**Line 1, opener (pick one):** their dated action moment (hearing, campaign day, vote, episode); their content with its date (post, Reel, article); a referral (`[Name] meinte, ich soll mich bei dir melden`) only with the referrer's explicit permission; a shared place or event (Bremen, a conference) that really happened. If the first sentence would work for anyone else, rewrite it.

**Line 2, their benefit:** one sentence in the founder's spoken first person that ends on what their people (or they) can do or have, tied to the line-1 moment (`Ich hab … gebaut, damit eure Leute …`); it is field 3 of the five-field check in `recipient-context.md`. Use concrete nouns: who receives it, what they hold, by when. Never input steps (`X eingeben, Y bekommen`), a feature list, or how it works; that comes after a yes or their question. Never a polished conditional without `ich` (`hätten eure Leute einen konkreten nächsten Schritt`): in the 2026-11-11 blind test such lines won on benefit and lost on voice. Name a worry or feeling only when they wrote or said it. No product history, no `wir` from a solo founder.

**What-it-is line:** only where the table below says so; at most one sentence, and in a DM a half-clause inside line 2: what it is, who acts, and `kostenlos` when true (`ein kostenloses Werkzeug, mit dem eure Leute …`). No steps, no feature list.

**Approach by recipient** (working rules, chosen in field 5 of the five-field check; record it as `hook_variant`; a project's `positioning.md` `## Message boundaries` or persona instance may set another approach for its ICP and wins):

| Recipient | Opener → line 2 | What-it-is line | Emotion, from what |
|---|---|---|---|
| NGO, initiative, campaigner | their dated moment → what their people get (`plain-benefit`) | half-clause | mild: their own words or their members' question, quoted |
| Creator | their content or comments → an example for their audience (`give-first`) | half-clause | their audience's question |
| Journalist, editor | the news for their audience (`editorial-pitch.md`) | one sentence after the news | none: facts, a consented protagonist |
| Podcast host | their episode point → an episode idea for their listeners (`give-first`) | one sentence | their listeners' frustration; the founder's story as one sentence of guest material, not the opener |
| Funder, official, politician | programme or remit fit → readiness (`what-it-is`) | one or two sentences | none: show preparation, not passion |
| Buyer or user | their signal → what it is → situation question (`what-it-is`) | one plain sentence so they can judge relevance at once; drop it only when `positioning.md` rules it out | their problem in their words; one clause of the founder's true experience |
| Met in person, acquaintance | the meeting or last exchange, with their sentence → what they get (`plain-benefit`) | none; they know it | warmth from the real relationship |

`story-first` (the founder's own story as opener) is a test variant for creators and podcast hosts only; compare it with the default in the founder's own batches.

**Close, ask ladder (exactly one easy question, answerable with yes, no, a date, or a name; never either-or, never "Feedback?" without a concrete object):**
1. Permission to give (`Soll ich dir den Beispielbrief/die Seite zeigen?`) or one short question about their situation. Default.
2. A narrow opinion question about their work.
3. A direct 15–20 minute conversation, only with concrete shared context or the founder's explicit goal plus a recipient-specific reason.
4. Share, intro, campaign, or coverage: only after a reply or the recipient's own offer. When they offered an intro or help, accept that offer directly.

No ask only for pure thank-yous, public comments, and warming touches. **Referral fallback for organisations:** `Falls das bei euch jemand anderes entscheidet, sag gern wer.` This is Thomas's confirmed pattern and part of the same ask, not a second one.

**Give-first asset:** the persona's `## Give-first asset`; offer only what exists or mark `[PRÜFEN: Asset gebaut?]`. Nothing unsolicited for funders.

**Length:**
- LinkedIn/Instagram first DM: aim for 50–70 words; at most about 90 with a what-it-is half-clause.
- Instagram message request to a non-follower: 80 words or fewer, text only.
- LinkedIn connection note: 200 characters or fewer, no ask.
- Email pitch: 120–180 words (reading budget in `editorial-pitch.md`).
- Warm email after an in-person meeting: 60–120 words; line 1 names the meeting and the topic discussed.
- Funder sketch: per `knowledge/personas/funders-foundations.md`.

**Kurz-Modus:** when the founder's brief ends with `kurz`, this overrides the length list, the line order, and a persona's `## Message skeleton`, for first touches, follow-ups, and replies alike; voice comes from `write-like-me` (its `Kurz` rule) or `workspace/voice.md`.
- Shape: greeting, two or three one-sentence lines (each its own paragraph), first-name sign-off. Body at most about 40 words; email subject two to five words.
- Line 1 = the opener from this card (dated moment, shared context, or the earlier exchange); line 2 = the one new fact; last line = the one ask from the ladder, or the link.
- Cut product explanation, proof line, give-first description, referral fallback, and PS. Gap markers, the recognition test, one ask, and the legal route still apply.
- Not for a funder sketch; there, say once that the sketch route sets its own form.

Example, follow-up after a meeting (synthetic): „Moin Eva, / weißt du noch, die Briefaktion vor der Anhörung? / Der Beispielbrief zu eurem Thema steht jetzt. / Soll ich ihn dir schicken? / Grüße aus Bremen, Thomas“

**No channel named:** use the contact's `primary_channel`, else the persona's default entry path, and make the channel one check item.

**Proof:** real social proof only, from the project's `## Gemeinsame Belegzeilen`.

**Pre-send check:** (1) recognition test: would they know from line one that this is about them? (2) line 2 = their benefit; (3) exactly one ask, plus the optional fallback; (4) no `wir`, product biography, input-step description, impact claim, or undefined number; (5) voice: read against `workspace/voice.md`, confirmed-native samples for this channel first; generic LinkedIn polish loses, and thanks, praise, or warmth only in patterns he confirmed there; (6) open markers listed, channel and legal route checked against `knowledge/markets/germany-eu.md` (`## Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach`).

**Examples** (synthetic; `[SIGNAL: …]`/`[PRÜFEN: …]` per `knowledge/foundations/human-writing.md`):

GOOD, NGO: „Moin Jana, ihr ruft gerade dazu auf, vor der Anhörung am 12.11. den Abgeordneten zu schreiben. Ich hab ein kostenloses, überparteiliches Werkzeug gebaut, damit eure Leute dafür nicht bei null anfangen: Jede:r hat am Ende einen eigenen Brief an die eigene Abgeordnete und schickt ihn selbst ab. Soll ich dir einen Beispielbrief zu eurem Thema schicken? Falls das bei euch jemand anderes entscheidet, sag gern wer. Grüße aus Bremen, Thomas“
→ Dated action moment; line 2 keeps his spoken `Ich hab … gebaut, damit eure Leute …` and ends on what they hold and who receives it; the what-it-is line is a half-clause; no input steps.

GOOD, creator: „Hey [Name], unter deinem Reel zu [SIGNAL: Thema, Datum] fragen einige, was sie jetzt konkret tun können [PRÜFEN: steht das so in den Kommentaren?]. Ich hab ein kostenloses Werkzeug gebaut, damit deine Leute darauf eine Antwort haben: einen eigenen Brief an die, die dafür zuständig sind. Mein Hintergedanke, ganz offen: dass du's irgendwann deinen Leuten zeigst. Darf ich dir einen Beispielbrief zu deinem Thema schicken? Thomas aus Bremen“
→ The emotion is their audience's own question; the benefit is for their audience; the openly named motive is the honest part of the ask; the asset costs nothing to accept.

GOOD, buyer/user (commercial project): „Moin [Name], du hast [SIGNAL: am … geschrieben, dass X jede Woche Zeit frisst]. Ich baue gerade allein [Projekt: was es ist, ein Satz in einfachen Wörtern], damit [PRÜFEN: Ergebnis aus `positioning.md`, in ihren Worten, keine Schrittfolge]. Wie löst ihr das im Moment? Grüße aus Bremen, Thomas“
→ Their words carry the problem; one plain sentence says what is being built, so they can judge relevance at once; a situation question instead of a demo ask.

BAD: „Hallo Frau X, ich verfolge Ihre spannende Arbeit schon lange! Wir haben eine innovative Civic-Tech-Lösung mit KI-Briefen, PLZ-Zuordnung und Kampagnenseiten entwickelt. Hätten Sie Zeit für einen 30-minütigen Call? Und würden Sie es mit Ihrem Netzwerk teilen?“
→ Generic compliment, `wir`, feature list, a call before relevance, and a second ask (share).

BAD, line 2: „Eure Leute geben PLZ und Anliegen ein und bekommen einen Briefentwurf, den sie überarbeiten und abschicken.“
→ Input steps describe the tool, not what they get; how it works comes after a yes.

## Boundaries

Before drafting, apply `recipient-context.md`: distinguish role from relationship, use one current public or recorded context point, and keep possible benefit and hesitation as internal hypotheses rather than claims about their motives in the copy. Choose the smallest relevant ask; a share, introduction, expert review, or call can impose substantial time or reputation cost. Read the matching channel and DE/US/cross-border reference. A LinkedIn connection is not evidence of a warm relationship or permission. Do not turn nationality or job title into a script.

The message must still make sense if the product name is removed. Use only proof recorded in the project evidence. Match the recipient's language and relationship temperature.

Do not hide the eventual purpose behind a personal opener. An apparent micro-ask that requires product review, free consulting, an introduction, a share, or a meeting is still a substantial request. Avoid full product explanations, generic compliments, several CTAs, fake urgency, and requests for a call before relevance is established.

Connection acceptance, a like, or silence does not establish interest. After a substantive reply, respond to what the person said before offering an example or suggesting a conversation. When the founder explicitly wants a conversation and a concrete recipient-specific context already establishes relevance, a transparent conversation invitation can be the first message's one ask. Do not replace that goal with a generic validation question. An answer by message may be offered as an alternative to the same invitation; it is not permission to add separate requests to test, share, or introduce. A direct editorial pitch to someone who expects story ideas follows `editorial-pitch.md` instead.

Warm paths and public presence can make a direct message less cold. A useful public comment, a visible market note, or a trusted introduction is worth more than more copy polish when the target would otherwise have no context for the founder.

## First-message coaching

For a cold LinkedIn first message, aim for a relevant, substantive exchange. Start with one real observation about the person's work and make the reason for writing clear. A specific, sincere appreciation may fit; compliments are optional, and the one question comes from the ask ladder. Do not recommend skill endorsements when the founder cannot assess those skills, or manufacture engagement as an obligation to reply.

When the founder requests a premature pitch, call, share, introduction, or substantial review, give one brief, concrete nudge explaining the mismatch between the requested effort and the actual relationship. Prepare one usable draft with the smallest fitting next move. Put the nudge in the existing rationale or next-action section; do not add a lecture, a checklist for the founder, or a second draft. If the ask already fits the context, simply draft it. Preserve the direct conversation and expected editorial-pitch exceptions above; do not replace a relevant invitation with a contrived question or disguise the real purpose behind small talk.

Treat outreach screenshots as ideas to test. A self-reported reply rate, a testimonial, or praise for the outreach technique does not prove product interest or an agreed next step. Check what the reply actually says before increasing the ask.

For triggered outbound, connect the observed signal to one concrete workflow bottleneck. The message should show:

1. What was observed.
2. Why that might create a timely problem.
3. One easy relevance check or permission ask from the ask ladder.

Do not treat funding, hiring, or a leadership change as proof that the buyer is shopping. They are only reasons to ask a better question now.

## Evidence boundary

Observational vendor email data favour an interest question or relevant offer over an immediate meeting request ([Gong 304,174-email CTA analysis](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings), [85-million-email guide](https://www.gong.io/files/gong-guide-how-to-master-cold-email-get-the-data-backed-guide-based-on-85-million-emails.pdf), reverified 2026-10-02) and link pitching in the body with up to 57% fewer replies ([Gong 28M-email page](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says), checked 2026-11-11). None isolates a no-ask first touch or transfers a sales cadence to German or civic outreach. No randomized test of benefit-first versus feature-first outreach copy was found; line 2 rests on field studies where a salient, concrete benefit and simple wording raised action (Bhargava and Manoli 2015) and on concrete wording signalling attention (Packard and Berger 2021), so it is a working rule. Stories persuade mass audiences slightly on average but raised counterarguing among US state legislators (Xu, Winett and Niederdeppe 2021); the only reply-level sentiment data favour mild warmth over neutral or strong emotion (Boomerang 2016, vendor). The one randomized length test with busy professionals found a 49-word email beat 127 words on clicks (Rogers and Lasky-Fink 2020); vendor reply data on length are weak and conflicting, so the word counts above are medium norms, not measured optima. Vendor LinkedIn data show similar acceptance with or without a note and isolate no warm-up comment, so the comment stays optional. Vendor "average reply rates" differ about tenfold; never set a percentage target, compare same-type messages in the founder's own batches via `hook_variant` and `reaction`.
