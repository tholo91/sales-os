# LinkedIn public posts

Use for a founder's own post, draft improvement, or review of observed post results. This is an editorial method, not a ranking formula. Invitation and DM wording stays in the channel adapter; this file only defines how post engagement becomes a warm path.

## Start from the material

Use the existing draft, rough notes, spoken account, or verified artifact. Recover audience and purpose from context before asking. Do not run an obligatory interview about tone, variants, CTA, and images when the input already answers those questions.

Privately identify the intended reader, the single point they should understand, and the source that earns that point. Preserve the founder's strongest original sentences. A reader benefit can be a useful distinction, honest experience, concrete example, or relevant announcement; it need not become a how-to list.

If the source is thin, still draft the complete post from recorded facts and mark open facts per `knowledge/foundations/human-writing.md` (`## Draft-first and gap markers`); the one question about the missing observation, decision, or artifact goes into the manual step. A full self-written draft is optional. Do not fill the gap with a fabricated conversation, metric, location, emotion, or customer problem.

## Meaning before hook

Before drafting, decide privately what the post is about: the metric (a number), the mechanism (how it happened), or the meaning (what it means to the founder or reader). Build line 1 from that choice. Founder feedback 2026-09-18: a hook built on a traffic spike missed the point; the meaning was that people recommended the product without the founder. A number can still appear, but in service of the chosen centre. A centre needs its material: organic recommendation needs a dated screenshot or message, a user voice needs a consented quote. Without it, write the premise that existing evidence supports (`core/steps/publish.md` step 4), not a post built around a placeholder.

Fertiges Asset may carry exactly one `Alternativer Einstieg:` line built from a different centre, with the body unchanged. No second full post, hook menu, or variant set.

## Choose a form that fits

| Material and purpose | Useful shape | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Personal experience | Real moment, what changed, why it matters | No manufactured crisis or obligatory life lesson |
| Result or useful explanation | Show the artifact, comparison, or answer, then explain its implication | A demonstration or mini-guide needs real material; no invented uplift |
| Reasoned position | Clear claim, basis, distinction or trade-off | Label opinion and uncertainty; no manufactured contrarianism |
| Genuine open question | Specific unresolved decision, relevant context, one answerable question | The uncertainty must be real; no empty agreement prompt |
| Announcement or invitation | What changed, who benefits, how to use or join it | State the offer clearly; no fake neutral lesson or urgency |

Choose one dominant purpose. These are editorial possibilities, not mandatory structures; `## Lead archetypes` maps them to who they attract. Select opening, length, paragraph flow, ending, and media separately for the material. A useful answer can begin with the answer; an argued position need not end in a lesson or question. Use the shared split check when two independent ideas compete; retain one asset unless a split is accepted.

## Lead archetypes

Ten founder archetypes. Project-specific hooks for them live in `workspace/projects/<slug>/hook-library.md`; fill only with real material.

| Archetype | Purpose | Attracts | Possible lead | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic recommendation | Show how the product spreads without the founder | Multipliers, media | "How did that happen?" | None, or one easy experience question |
| Responsibility explainer (carousel) | Answer a question many people get wrong | Educators, organisations | Request to use it | Save or send it |
| Deliberate product decision | Explain what the product does not do and why | Cautious organisations, funders | Fit conversation | One real question |
| Error and fix | One real mistake and what changed | Peers, funders | Expert feedback, intro | None |
| User voice (with consent) | One real message or review, dated | General audience, media | Journalist wants a protagonist | None |
| Number with definition | One number plus what it counts and what it does not | Media, funders | Data request, funding chat | Question about the definition |
| Reasoned position | Labelled opinion with its basis | Debaters, podcasters | Podcast or panel invite | None or one question |
| Current occasion | News moment, who is responsible, next step | People affected, creators covering it | Creator shares a link | One link |
| Invitation to organisations | What organisations can do with the product now | Organisation staff | Setup call | One link or "schreibt mir kurz" |
| Concrete ask | Who, what, effort, in the preview | Helpers, funders, intros | Intro or pilot | The ask itself |

Alternate a reach archetype with a lead archetype (number, invitation, ask). Never publish two asks in a row.

## Find ideas when requested

Read the available active-project evidence, founder notes, real questions, recent decisions, or artifacts. Look for something worth saying to the intended audience rather than filling generic content pillars. A source can be a product decision or useful distinction; do not demand a dramatic personal event.

Offer up to three different premises, not three hook rewrites. Each should name the angle, its factual anchor or required confirmation, the reader benefit, and a suitable treatment. Preserve the source's known status: an existing or verified artifact is not automatically published, public, or approved to share. Do not invent a release, link, or public availability when suggesting an angle. Recommend the strongest grounded premise for the current goal. When fewer good premises exist, offer fewer. If context is genuinely thin, mark the missing confirmation on the premise rather than inventing achievements or customer demand.

When the founder gives a concrete idea, choose the best treatment and draft directly unless they ask to compare options. If their idea already has a clear shape, respect it.

If recent posts are available, inspect a small relevant sample for repeated opening tricks, emotional arcs, bullet layouts, and final questions. Vary a suitable treatment where it helps the new point. Do not impose alternating formats, novelty quotas, or a permanent creator style; preserve the founder's cadence. Unknown post history is not a blocker and must not be fabricated.

## Edit the opening and ending together

Start with the most relevant concrete sentence. Helpful details can answer who, what happened, where, when, or what was at stake; use only those that advance this point. Do not impose a detail quota or force a monetary cost into every hook.

The opening creates an expectation that the body must fulfill. A clear answer can be stronger than withheld information. Reject vague suspense, unsupported superlatives, and claims whose scope exceeds the source. Preview length is covered in `## First lines`.

End on the same point. A callback can work when natural. Choose a statement, a specific question, or one relevant action. Zero CTA is valid. Count a promotional PS, newsletter invitation, download request, and comment request as actions too; do not hide a second ask below the post.

If the aim is discussion, ask something the intended reader can answer from experience. If the aim is use of an existing resource, give a direct path when available. Avoid empty agreement prompts and keyword comments used merely to inflate activity. A real request to receive a resource is not proof of buying intent and never authorizes automated DMs.

## First lines

- One plain sentence says what the product does for the reader; a stranger cannot care how something spreads or counts if they cannot picture it.
- Name the topic in plain words in lines 1–3. LinkedIn ranks with LLM embeddings of posts and members, so the topic must be unambiguous; stay within one main topic and two or three related ones that match the profile.
- Include one detail only this founder can know: a dated observation, decision, screenshot, consented user message, or number with its definition. Delete sentences that could stand on any founder's profile. LinkedIn reports cutting views of AI slop by 40%.
- Practitioner check, not a rule: line 1 about 70 characters or fewer, lines 1+2 about 140 or fewer, because German compounds fill the mobile preview fast. The promise is complete before "mehr anzeigen"; check the phone preview.
- Prefer one concrete scene (place, object, a sentence someone said) over a contrast slogan.
- Avoid: "Das ist nicht X. Das ist Y.", "weniger X, mehr Y" openers, staccato fragment chains ("Ein Brief. Handgeschrieben. Echt."), punchline closers, "Unpopuläre Meinung:", "Niemand redet darüber", "Stell dir vor", emoji chains, rhetorical questions the text never answers.

## Voice, layout, links, and media

- Edit in the founder's demonstrated channel and language. Preserve ordinary nouns, rhythm, humor, and uncertainty; never borrow a creator's profanity, vulnerability, revenue, or authority.
- Use readable paragraphs and varied sentence length. Stop when the point is clear; no fixed ideal length or line-by-line suspense format.
- Place links per `## Links`. Hashtags are optional topic labels, not a required growth tactic.
- Add a real photo, screenshot, or diagram when it provides evidence or helps explain the point. Text-only is a valid choice. A before/after visual should make its comparison legible on a phone; avoid copying a dense desktop graphic into the feed.
- Recommend a document carousel only when several pages improve understanding enough to justify the work. Do not generate fake product screenshots, user quotes, or images presented as evidence of a real event. Verify current upload requirements when producing an asset; format preferences are design choices, not performance guarantees.

## Links

Decide per goal and record the variant in the experiment log. Reach post: no link; point to the profile's Featured section or name the site as plain text. Action post: one link at the end, accepting a possible reach cost. Do not move a link into a comment on an unverified reach claim. The practitioner evidence conflicts (see `## Evidence and limits`).

## Partner posts

Mention real partners only when they appear in the post and agreed beforehand ("Darf ich euch erwähnen?"); at most one or two mentions, never tags for reach. Tagged people are a feed signal and get notified, so a consented post about a partner's real use is often the strongest lead post. After publication, a one-line note that it is live is enough.

## Final editorial check

1. **Ownership:** Is this this founder's perspective, and does it sound like their original material?
2. **Focus:** Can the main point and reader relevance each be stated in one sentence, and does one sentence say what the product does?
3. **Evidence:** Are details supported, dated where needed, and scoped correctly? Created, sent, viewed, and purchased are different outcomes. A number a page displays is quoted exactly as displayed.
4. **Delivery:** Does the body fulfill the opening's promise with something concrete?
5. **Ending:** Does the ending belong to that point, with zero or one intended action?

Repair a weak check through a small edit or a marked gap. Do not expose a scoring rubric or extra drafts unless requested; keep the skill's normal handoff.

## Cadence and comment routine

Two posts a week is a founder-capacity plan, not a ranking claim; a third only with real material, never a filler post. Post only when the founder can stay about 60 minutes and reply to every substantive comment with substance, not "Danke!".

Comments on other people's posts are the main distribution and warm-up channel. The pipeline board sets the weekly budget (10 substantive comments); a good day is 3–5 in 10–15 minutes on posts by people from the board or priority list. Each adds a fact, counter-example, or question from the founder's experience; no link, no product mention unless asked, no restating the post. Use `warm-contact-comment` for a specific target. No pods, automation, or "Kommentiere X"-CTAs.

## Comment-to-DM bridge

Signal ladder, strongest first: own case or question in a comment > repost with own words > substantive comment > likes. Never DM likers or profile viewers.

1. Reply publicly first, with substance, within the hour.
2. DM within 24–48 h only when the comment, or their reply to Thomas's public answer, states a concrete need or explicit interest; a role fit alone means public reply only. Draft it with `draft-outreach` as a warm, post-originated touch.
3. The DM quotes or paraphrases the comment and makes one small offer or asks one question. Never "Ich habe deinen Kommentar gesehen" as the whole reason, never a keyword-for-resource trade.
4. Not connected: send a note-free connection request when the public exchange makes the context obvious; spend invitation notes only on top multipliers, because free accounts get very few (limit in `knowledge/markets/germany-eu.md`, `## Rechtliche Kurzreferenz für Outreach`). DM after acceptance.
5. Add the person as a Tier-2 row on the pipeline board with `source_post` set to the post reference.

## Profile as landing page

Curious readers check the profile, not the website. The headline names the project and what it does in plain German. Featured holds the site, the organisation or campaign page, and the best explainer post. About ends with one sentence on who should write and about what. Fix the profile before raising post volume; it also gives reach posts a link-free CTA.

## Learn from an actual post

Use the optional public-post review in the existing `experiments.md`, not a second tracking system. Before publication, identify one desired outcome appropriate to the project: a useful reply, relevant visibility, resource use, introduction, or payment-related action. Do not force a sales goal onto a free or civic project.

After the founder confirms publication, record the post reference, observation time, distribution context, intended audience, and actual goal-relevant result. Keep unknown values blank or `not recorded`. Available diagnostics may include reach, audience demographics, saves, sends, profile visits, and external-link visits. These are estimated platform measures; own activity can count, and audience data may be unavailable. Clicks do not establish use or payment.

Compare posts at a similar elapsed time and record paid promotion or unusual external amplification. Change one deliberate variable in the next comparable post. A few posts produce a useful hypothesis, not a causal experiment or a performance guarantee. Actual replies belong in the normal interaction and learning flow.

## Timing as a test

LinkedIn lists content recency among many feed signals; this does not reveal a universal best publication hour. Buffer's original analysis finds engagement differences by posting time in posts sent through its service. It is observational and does not isolate timing from account, topic, audience, or format, or forecast this founder's results.

When advice is requested, prefer available account observations and the intended audience's timezone. Also consider whether the topic is timely and when the founder can realistically respond. Recommend one feasible window with a short rationale, marking an untested window as a hypothesis. Without account evidence, avoid presenting an aggregate peak as a personalized optimum; do not hold a timely post for a supposed magic slot.

For a timing test, record actual publication time and timezone, compare similar posts at a similar elapsed age, and vary the window while keeping treatment and audience reasonably comparable. Do not change timing, format, and topic together and attribute the result to timing. Being available for genuine replies is a practical choice, not proof of a mandatory first-hour algorithm boost. The 2-posts-per-week baseline is a founder-capacity plan, not a ranking claim; never schedule or publish.

## Evidence and limits

LinkedIn describes relevance to professional interests and efforts to reduce repetitive, low-substance content and engagement bait. It permits AI writing assistance while asking for the member's own voice and perspective, and describes reduced distribution of generic AI content. These are platform statements, not disclosed ranking weights or a forecast for an individual post.

LinkedIn also describes a ranker built on LLM embeddings and each member's reading history, refreshed within minutes of new engagement, and lists profile identity and tagged people among feed signals.
