# Public publishing step

1. Recover the platform, intended audience, communication or learning goal, and truthful source idea from the founder's draft, notes, and active context. Never block on a question: draft from recorded facts, mark gaps per `knowledge/foundations/human-writing.md` (`## Draft-first and gap markers`), and put the one question in the manual step.
2. Read active positioning, project evidence, matching founder voice, `knowledge/foundations/short-punchy-content.md`, and only the relevant platform or community guidance. For LinkedIn public posts also read `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`. Verify current rules when they materially affect the draft.
   When the founder requests ideas, use available evidence to propose up to three distinct premises with source anchors, reader benefits, and suitable treatments; recommend one. Flag missing confirmations without fabricating them. Return this idea packet inside the normal handoff for an explicit ideas-only request; when the founder asked for a post rather than only ideas, draft the recommended premise in the same turn. Leave draft-ready state and unrelated pending actions untouched.
3. Choose one premise: a concrete scene, observation, result, failed attempt, useful artifact, or honest question. Before drafting, check whether the source contains two independent scenes, lessons, audiences, or desired actions. Recommend two focused posts when it does; do not combine several strong ideas into one generic thought-leadership post.
4. Choose an experience, result or explanation, reasoned position, genuine open question, or announcement based on the source and goal. Choose the opening, length, layout, ending, and media accordingly; do not run every idea through the same story arc. If recent posts are available, check for mechanical repetition without imposing a rotation. Make the reader benefit clear within the post. A relevant announcement may name the product immediately; do not disguise promotion as a neutral lesson or manufacture a scene to introduce it.
   Meaning before hook: decide privately whether the post is about the metric, the mechanism, or the meaning, and build line 1 from it; name the topic in plain words in lines 1–3 and include one founder-only detail. Pick the centre from material that exists (a quote, screenshot, message, or a Belegzeile). When the requested premise needs recorded material that does not exist (no quote for "what people report back", no screenshot or message for "people recommend it"), never put a placeholder or an unmarked claim at its core: draft the nearest premise that existing evidence supports, name that premise and its source in one routing line, and name in `Nächste Aktion:` the one artifact that would make the requested premise postable. Before claiming organic spread, check the period against the coverage dates in `## Gemeinsame Belegzeilen`.
   Every post says in one plain sentence what the product does for the reader (project sentence in `hook-library.md` when present). One concrete scene (place, object, a sentence someone said) beats a contrast slogan; scope caveats are one plain sentence. Quote a number a page displays exactly as displayed, with its date, never "heute". At most one `Alternativer Einstieg:` line from a different centre, body unchanged; omit it when no second centre has material.
5. Draft in the supported platform's native form. For Reddit, respect the exact community rules; for LinkedIn, preserve the founder's real cadence, strongest original lines, and specificity. Check that the body delivers the opening's promise and the ending belongs to the same point. Instagram Reels follow draft-reel and `knowledge/channels/instagram-reels.md`; a TikTok script reuses that shape only on explicit request, with `knowledge/channels/tiktok.md` disclosure rules; other platforms without an adapter get a plain draft labelled untested.
6. Use zero or one CTA that fits the goal. Count questions, links framed as actions, and PS invitations together. Replace generic hooks with a specific one from the source; remove engagement bait, unsupported trends, and ornamental hashtags. No keyword-comment, "Teil das", or tag-for-reach CTAs. Choose links and media for reader usefulness; hook-length figures are a phone-preview check, not a cutoff; no optimal length or format advantage without current evidence. For LinkedIn, decide the link per goal (`## Links` in the strategy file).
   If timing advice is requested or materially relevant, use the intended audience's timezone, available account evidence, the topic's urgency, and founder availability. Recommend one feasible window as a hypothesis; do not append a universal best time or schedule anything. For a timing-only request, keep the finished draft and return the recommendation rather than a new post.
7. Return one final asset by default, the manual publishing step, and the exact result to bring back. If the founder accepts a split, return two clearly separate assets with their own openings, payoffs, and endings. A draft with open gap markers is not ready to publish. Do not publish, schedule, or generate a content calendar by default.
8. After confirmed manual publication, record the attempt and the goal-relevant outcome in the existing project experiment log. Treat substantive replies as interactions and route them through reply handling and learning capture. Substantive commenters and reposters in a target role become Tier-2 rows on the pipeline board with source_post; their DMs go through draft-outreach after a public reply (bridge rules in `knowledge/strategies/linkedin-public-post.md`). For performance comparisons record observation time and organic/paid context; treat reach metrics as diagnostic context, not sales proof. Change one deliberate variable in the next comparable post and treat small samples as directional evidence.
   Record actual publication time and timezone when comparing posting windows.
