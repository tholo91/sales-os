# Public publishing step

1. Confirm the platform, intended audience, communication or learning goal, and one truthful source idea the founder can own.
2. Read active positioning, project evidence, matching founder voice, `knowledge/foundations/short-punchy-content.md`, and only the relevant platform or community guidance. Verify current rules when they materially affect the draft.
3. Choose one premise: a concrete scene, observation, result, failed attempt, useful artifact, or honest question. Before drafting, check whether the source contains two independent scenes, lessons, audiences, or desired actions. Recommend two focused posts when it does; do not combine several strong ideas into one generic thought-leadership post.
4. Give the audience a stand-alone payoff before any product or service mention. Connect the post to the commercial learning goal without disguising a pitch as neutral participation.
5. Draft in the supported platform's native form. For Reddit, respect the exact community rules; for LinkedIn, preserve the founder's real cadence and specificity. Stop at a channel gate for TikTok, Instagram, or any platform without a maintained adapter and evaluation path.
6. Use at most one CTA aimed at a useful reply, relevant introduction, or next conversation. Remove generic hooks, engagement bait, unsupported trends, and ornamental hashtags.
7. Return one final asset by default, the manual publishing step, and the exact result to bring back. If the founder accepts a split, return two clearly separate assets with their own hooks, payoffs, and questions. Do not publish, schedule, or generate a content calendar by default.
8. After manual publication, record the attempt. Treat substantive replies as interactions and route them through reply handling and learning capture; treat reach metrics as diagnostic context, not sales proof.
