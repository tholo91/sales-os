# Offer preparation step

1. Read the selected proposal action and its `target_ref`, contact and interaction history, active `positioning.md`, project evidence, and `offer.md`. Confirm why an offer is appropriate now.
2. Extract the buyer's current situation, desired outcome, constraints, decision participants, timing, and evidence of urgency or willingness to commit. Name buyer, user, and payer separately when they differ. Mark missing material facts as assumptions or ask one clarification.
3. Choose the smallest engagement that can credibly produce the agreed outcome. Prefer a bounded paid service or pilot with a decision point over a broad menu or speculative roadmap.
4. Define the outcome, included work, exclusions, delivery sequence, responsibilities on both sides, timing, price and payment shape, success or acceptance condition, and the next decision.
5. Tie price and scope to the recorded outcome and delivery reality. Change scope, timing, risk, or payment shape before offering an unsupported discount.
6. Remove invented savings, proof, urgency, legal terms, tax claims, procurement assumptions, and deliverables the founder has not confirmed they can provide.
7. Produce one concise offer that can be shared or discussed without a separate explanatory pitch. Use multiple options only when recorded buyer constraints genuinely require a choice.
8. External sharing, negotiation, acceptance, signing, invoicing, and payment remain manual. Keep the proposal action open until the founder confirms sharing or cancellation, then preserve that outcome as an interaction record, resolve only that action, and set the contact stage accordingly. Route questions or objections to reply handling and acceptance or rejection to learning capture. Create a `decision_follow_up` action only from a real decision date and reason, and include the originating `source_interaction_ref` and due date.
