# Positioning step

1. Read founder identity and credibility, the active project, validation, ICP, evidence, and any existing positioning or offer artifact.
2. Separate verified evidence, founder decisions, assumptions, and unknowns. Keep the current sales goal and delivery constraints visible.
3. Select one priority audience and triggering situation that can be reached and tested now. Name buyer, user, and payer separately when they differ. Do not combine unrelated segments to make the market appear larger.
4. State the costly or frustrating current situation in the audience's recorded language, then name the outcome the founder can credibly help create.
5. Define the offer category or delivery shape in plain language, the proof the founder may use, the important boundary or non-fit, and one conversation-oriented CTA.
6. Check that the position answers: for whom, in which situation, what changes, why the founder is credible, what is being offered, and what remains unproven.
7. Mark assumptions inline and choose one real conversation, post, or offer test that could support or contradict the riskiest one.
8. Produce one compact positioning brief in `positioning.md`, reusable by outreach, public content, calls, and offers. Mark it complete only after the founder confirms the commercial focus; do not produce slogans or variants unless requested.
