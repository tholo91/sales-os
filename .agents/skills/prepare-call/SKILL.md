# Prepare Call

Use the call to understand one decision and earn one truthful next commitment.

## Gate

Require a real contact and scheduled, offered, or explicitly requested conversation context. The only exception is explicit practice mode, which must name the real upcoming conversation or label every hypothetical buyer fact as an assumption.

## Required references

- `core/steps/call.md`
- Active project evidence and the contact's interaction history
- `knowledge/foundations/mindset.md` only for nerves or avoidance
- Pricing guidance only for pilot or price conversations

## Safety boundary

Do not invent participant pain, authority, objections, budget, or interest. Do not turn discovery into a hidden demo. In practice mode, never record role-play statements as customer evidence or imply that a simulated objection is likely.

## Modes

- **Validation:** test one problem, behavior, or willingness-to-change assumption without hiding a pitch inside the interview.
- **Discovery:** understand the last real occurrence, current workaround, consequences, decision process, and switching condition.
- **Demo:** anchor one proof moment in the buyer's stated workflow and define what not to show.
- **Pilot:** agree use case, owners, duration, success and stop conditions, price, and decision date.
- **Pricing:** connect one price to the recorded outcome, diagnose resistance, preserve scope boundaries, and ask for one decision.
- **Practice:** rehearse a real upcoming moment or an assumption-labeled scenario, respond as the buyer only from supplied context, then give concise feedback and the next round.

## Output contract

Return exactly these four sections in the founder's language:

1. `Nächste Aktion:` name the call mode and the single objective.
2. `Fertiges Asset:` the ready-to-use plan or current practice turn. Include only the mode-relevant questions, proof, boundaries, likely resistance, close, and evidence to capture.
3. `Dein manueller Schritt:` conduct the call or answer the current rehearsal turn.
4. `Sag mir danach:` provide the real outcome, exact language, commitment, objection, or next practice response.

Keep discovery plans to five behavior-first questions. For other modes, do not force five questions when a smaller decision plan is clearer.
